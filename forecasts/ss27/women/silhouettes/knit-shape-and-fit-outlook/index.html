SS27 Women's Knit Shape and Fit Outlook

Knitwear moves into tailoring and lingerie. Sheer, single-layer knits replace the light jacket, ribbed knits get longer and closer, and crochet and open-stitch knits return in oversized cuts.

The evidence

The most important knit idea of SS27 came from tailoring houses rather than knit specialists. At Louis Vuitton, Nicolas Ghesquière cut diaphanous tailoring from the lightest knits, designed to be worn as one layer: "We needed to think differently. You want one layer"[1]. Hermès showed sheer knits and streamlined bandeau tops[1][2], and Chanel's 83-look collection included sheer knits[2]. Courrèges, under Drew Henry's first collection, showed a knitted bandeau with cartoon poppies[1].

Heavier knits did not disappear; they changed role. Prada paired its skirts with chunky knits, blazers and leather jackets[2]. Bottega Veneta and Brunello Cucinelli showed crochet and open-stitch styles in oversized cuts, and florals moved onto knitwear at Stine Goya and Monse[3].

At fabric level, Première Vision's SS27 knit selection points the same way: flat, Derby and Richelieu ribs that are more elastic and elongated than jersey, and a retro-sport family of honeycomb piqués, plated knits, diamond and wheat stitches in slightly yellowed, sepia-like tones[4]. Its season overview adds cool wools, extra-fine merino and combed mohair[5].

Why it matters

Heat changed the brief. Designers designed for one layer[1], which makes fine knits a replacement for the light jacket and the shirt rather than an extra layer on top. Knit also suits the season's separates logic: a knit top works with every skirt shape in the collection. And the lingerie trend, with bras and bandeaus shown as tops[1], gives knitwear a new, small but visible category: the knitted bandeau and bra top.

Shapes and fit

Knit as tailoring. A fine-gauge knit jacket or cardigan-jacket, cut like a blazer, unlined, worn as the only layer. Fit: close at the shoulder, straight or slightly waisted body, hip length.

Sheer fine-gauge layers. Transparent long-sleeved tops and tunics in fine merino or silk blends, worn over a bra top or slip. Fit: close to the body; length either at the hip or tunic length over a skirt.

The elongated rib. Polo and crew-neck ribs cut longer and closer, using the stretch and recovery of Derby and Richelieu ribs. Fit: second-skin through the body, tucked into high-waisted skirts.

The knitted bandeau and bra top. A small but visible category in SS27. Fit is technical: it needs grip at the top edge, a firm rib or elastic, and sizing by bust rather than S to XL.

Crochet and open stitch, oversized. Loose, dropped-shoulder shapes worn over slips and swimwear. Fit: generous, with weight-bearing seams so the fabric does not grow.

The chunky knit over a skirt. A short, boxy jumper over a long skirt balances the season's narrow and full skirts.

Timing

In stores February to July 2027. Sheer and one-layer knits are rising now and should carry into early AW27 as transitional layers. Crochet is a returning, seasonal idea: strongest for the high-summer drop. Knitted bra tops are at an early stage: test small.

Products and customers

  • Tops: sheer fine-gauge tops and tunics; elongated rib polos and crews; knitted bandeaus and bra tops; short-sleeved knit shirts.
  • Layers: fine knit jackets and cardigan-jackets; pointelle cardigans; cropped cardigans.
  • Dresses: long rib column dresses; crochet and open-stitch dresses over slips.
  • Customers: the separates customer who wants one piece to wear alone in heat; younger customers for bra tops and crochet.

Design implications

  • Choose the gauge before the shape. Fine gauges (14 to 18) hold close, tailored shapes and sheerness; chunky gauges need room and short lengths.
  • Design sheer pieces as a pair. A sheer knit needs a planned under-layer: a slip, bandeau or bra top in the same colour family.
  • Use rib for fit, not just trims. Derby and Richelieu ribs stretch and recover more than jersey[4], so they can replace darts and seams on fitted tops.
  • Stabilise open stitches. Tape shoulders and necklines on crochet and open-stitch knits so they keep their shape.
  • Bra tops need lingerie construction. Use firm top edges, elastic and, where needed, light cups. Consider working with a lingerie maker.

Commercial implications

  • Re-assign the light-jacket budget. Part of the unlined jacket buy can move into knit jackets and cardigan-jackets that work as one layer.
  • Pair knits with skirts in the range plan. Every core skirt shape should have at least one matching knit top in the same colour story.
  • Keep bra tops and crochet as short runs. High interest, smaller audience; buy them as tests and reorder winners.
  • Watch returns on sheer pieces. Product pages need clear photography of what is worn underneath.
