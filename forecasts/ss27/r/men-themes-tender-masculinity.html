SS27 Men's Tender Masculinity

Softness, sensuality and decoration in menswear: nude and aquamarine tones, lingerie references, organza feathers and floral satins.

Season
Spring/Summer 2027
Audience
Men
Category
Themes
Published
2026-11-25
Research period
Milan, Paris and Pitti Uomo menswear shows, June 2026; Premiere Vision Paris, February 2026; analysis July to November 2026
Market
Global: Milan, Paris and Florence runways; premium and mid-market menswear
Trend status
Emerging

The story

Menswear embraced softness and sensuality in Paris: Dries Van Noten showed sensual silhouettes in nude and aquamarine tones and Egonlab lingerie-inspired ensembles. Patrick Clark, fashion editor at GQ France, called the season "a confirmation of the crossover between the men's and women's wardrobes". At Pitti Uomo, Simone Rocha's first men's show used boas of organza feathers, cornflower-blue embellishment, aprons and pinafores, under the theme "tender masculinity". Willy Chavarria showed floral satin sets, and Thom Browne put pleated skirts on men in Milan.

Colours

Salmon pink, butter and ice blue, with nude and aquamarine tones. See SS27 Men's Season Colours.

Silhouettes

  • Fluid shirts and trousers; floral satin sets.
  • Skirts and aprons layered over trousers (fashion customers).
  • Soft, unstructured jackets.

Fabrics

Satin, including floral satin; organza; sheer cottons; fine knits.

Trims and details

Embroidered florals, organza feathers, brooches, beading.

Timing

In stores February to July 2027. An emerging direction: test with fashion customers; Forecut expects softer colour and fabric to reach the mainstream before skirts do.

Products and customers

  • Satin shirts and sets; embroidered shirts; soft-coloured knitwear; skirts and aprons for fashion customers.

Design implications

  • Introduce softness through colour and fabric first (satin, pastel knits).
  • Keep construction masculine where the customer needs it: shoulders, collars, trouser blocks.

Commercial implications

  • Small buys for skirts and aprons; build depth in satin shirts and soft-coloured knits.
  • Merchandise by mood, not by gender, for fashion stores and online.
