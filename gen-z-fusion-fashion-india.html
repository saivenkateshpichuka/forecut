Gen Z fusion: how India's youngest shoppers mix ethnic and street

Short kurtas over jeans, sarees with sneakers, jackets over lehengas and baggy denim: how Gen Z is changing Indian fashion, and what brands should do about it.

By Forecut Editorial · 11 October 2026 · 6 min read

India's youngest shoppers do not separate ethnic and western wear. They wear a short kurta over jeans to college, sarees with sneakers to a friend's sangeet, and an embroidered jacket over a lehenga. Forecut tracks this as Gen Z fusion, one of the five themes shaping Indian fashion in 2026–27.

Why it matters commercially

According to reporting by Outlook Business, Gen Z makes up roughly 40–45% of India's online shoppers and drove almost half of new orders in 2025. They discover styles on social video and buy fast, often through quick-commerce apps. That makes Gen Z the fastest feedback loop in Indian fashion.

The key looks

  • Kurtas with jeans and sneakers: the everyday uniform.
  • Sarees with sneakers and jackets over lehengas: festive, but on their own terms.
  • Baggy and wide-leg jeans, oversized tees and shirts: the western base layer.
  • Athleisure co-ord sets: comfort for travel, college and work-from-anywhere.
  • Gender-fluid, boxy tailoring and varsity jackets for menswear.

Who is showing it

Gully Labs builds everyday leather sneakers around Indian colour stories, while designers such as Payal Pratap and Jubinav showed embroidered jackets and sporty knitwear in 2026. Pearl Academy's Gen Z trend reports describe the same mix of heritage and streetwear.

How to win with Gen Z

  • Style every ethnic hero piece with sneakers and denim in your product imagery.
  • Launch small, fast drops and watch social signals every week.
  • Make sure products are listed on quick-commerce and marketplace apps, not only on your own site.
  • Compete on speed, size range and social-ready styling rather than heavy work, since simple shapes keep costs low.
