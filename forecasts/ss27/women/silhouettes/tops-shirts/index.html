The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Elegant ease

Dressing gets refined again. The tie returns, shirts are tucked in, trousers are pleated and slightly puffed, and elegant mules and pointed flats replace sneakers.

Why now

Fabric forecasters say street style and sneakers are giving way to elegance.

Seen at

  • Loewe: The return of the tie
  • Munich Fabric Start forecasters: "Everything is becoming elegant"
  • SS27 menswear: Slimmer ties, pleated and puffed trousers

Key pieces

Slim ties, Mules, Pointed flats; tucked shirts with slim ties.

Source: FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage and SS27 trend report

Undone

A deliberately unfinished look: straps falling off the shoulder, exposed or unhooked undergarments, top layers left unsewn and raw, frayed hems on muslin-like cottons.

Why now

A relaxed, personal take on elegance, and raw edges suit natural fabrics.

Seen at

  • Altuzarra: Deshabille looks
  • Christian Cowan: Undone layers
  • Coach and Prabal Gurung: Muslin looks
  • LII: Double-layer minis
  • Paloma Wool: Dropped linings

Key pieces

Raw hems, Slip straps, Muslin; unfinished raw hems.

Source: The Zoe Report, NYFW SS27 trends; FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage

Handcraft

Lace flowers, embroidery and smocking move onto crisp, everyday shirting. Craft techniques such as wrapping, gathering, pleating and openwork feel personal and slow.

Why now

Lace is named a growth category, and craft adds perceived value to simple poplin and knits.

Seen at

  • Chloé: Embroidery and waxed jackets
  • Gabriela Hearst: Mirrorwork inspired by Afghan craftsmanship
  • Lace (category): Forecasters call it a growth category

Key pieces

Lace, Smocking, Embroidery; lace-embroidered poplin shirts.

Source: FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage and SS27 trend report

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Backless apron tops

Halter tops that tie at the neck and leave the back open. Changing the neckline is one of the cheapest ways to update a proven block.

Design notes

  • Add silicone tape or internal stays so the garment stays in place.
  • Check neck opening against head circumference on pull-on styles.
  • Stay-stitch or tape the neckline so it does not stretch out.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Band-collar shirts with long placket

Collarless shirts with an extended placket. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.
  • Use a firm fusible or interlining so the collar holds its shape after washing.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Cropped boxy shirts

Short camp-collar shirts with straight hems. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Grade extra ease carefully so smaller sizes do not swamp.
  • Check the crop length against high-rise bottoms.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Double-pocket camp shirts

Camp-collar shirts with two chest pockets. Pockets add function and visual interest, and are easy to vary across a range.

Design notes

  • Reinforce pocket corners with bar tacks.
  • Check pocket placement against hand position on the fit model.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Epaulette shirts

Shoulder tabs on utility shirts. The shoulder line sets the silhouette and how the garment drapes.

Design notes

  • Keep classic trench details (storm flap, epaulettes, belt) but simplify proportions for a modern look.
  • Keep utility pockets flat and tidy for city wear; avoid too much bulk.
  • Adjust shoulder pads or remove them to match the silhouette.

Fabric and trims

Cotton gabardine, coated cotton or water-repellent twill.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Gilded embroidered shirting

Metallic embroidery on crisp poplin. Embellishment lifts price perception and suits occasion and gifting drops.

Design notes

  • Place embroidery on flat areas; use backing to avoid puckering.
  • Test metallic finishes for scratching and cracking.
  • Test embellishment for snagging and washing.

Fabric and trims

Metallic leather or foil-coated fabric.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Backless apron tops
  • Band-collar shirts with long placket
  • Cropped boxy shirts
  • Double-pocket camp shirts
  • Epaulette shirts
  • Gilded embroidered shirting

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Trompe-l'oeil shirt-print tops
  • Asymmetric-collar pinstripe shirts
  • Henley plackets
  • Peplum blouses
  • Vintage poster-print tops
  • Wrap and tie-front tops

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Contrast collars and cuffs
  • Floppy oversized collars
  • Ruched centre fronts
  • Rugby stripes, short plackets
  • Sheer mesh layering tops
  • Striped Breton tops

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Nothing at this stage

Actions

  • Add slim ties and pocket squares as add-on sales.
  • Style shirts tucked into pleated trousers in imagery.
  • Shift footwear mix from sneakers toward mules and flats.
  • Test raw hems in wash trials before bulk.
  • Layer a longer slip under existing dresses.
  • Style straps off-shoulder in imagery, not just in the garment.
