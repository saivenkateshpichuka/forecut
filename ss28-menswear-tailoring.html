The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Commuter tailoring

Suits built for city days: stretch suiting, soft shoulders, concealed buttons and easy-care fabrics.

Why now

Hybrid working needs tailoring that is comfortable on a commute and smart at a desk.

Key pieces

Stretch suits, Concealed buttons, Soft shoulders; stretch commuter suiting.

Source: Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Natural tech

Natural fibres finished to perform: quick-dry linen, UV-resistant cotton and easy-care jersey.

Why now

Customers want natural fibres without the creasing and care trouble.

Key pieces

Quick-dry, Easy-care, Natural fibres; performance-jersey shirt dresses.

Source: Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Performance ease

Designs that feel intuitive and move easily: articulated sleeves and knees, stretch and breathable fabrics for sport and commuting.

Why now

Early SS28 previews point to performance design that removes restriction.

Key pieces

Articulation, Stretch, Breathable; articulated sleeves.

Source: FashionUnited, "SS28 Preview: The modern flow of performance design". Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Check sports jackets

Checked jackets worn with odd trousers. The fabric story carries this detail, so sampling and handle are key.

Design notes

  • Match checks at centre front, pockets and side seams; plan for the extra fabric.
  • Use laser finishing for consistent, lower-impact distressing.
  • Request hand-feel swatches and test for pilling, shrinkage and colour fastness.

Fabric and trims

Yarn-dyed wool or brushed cotton checks; matching adds about 10–15% fabric.

Where to use it

Best on suits, blazers, waistcoats and smart trousers. Works in menswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Cropped suit trousers

Suit trousers cut above the ankle. Length shifts are simple to apply across existing blocks.

Design notes

  • Keep the jacket and trousers in the same cloth so they sell as a set.
  • Check the crop length against high-rise bottoms.
  • Update length on existing blocks rather than new patterns.

Fabric and trims

Tropical wool, wool-viscose suiting or linen blends, with half-canvas fronts and horn buttons.

Where to use it

Best on suits, blazers, waistcoats and smart trousers. Works in menswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Knitted ties

Slim knitted ties. An accessory-led detail that adds styling value at low cost.

Design notes

  • Make accessories detachable where possible.
  • Match finishes with other trims.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Mandarin-collar jackets

Tailored jackets with band collars. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.
  • Use a firm fusible or interlining so the collar holds its shape after washing.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Matching waistcoat three-pieces

Three-piece suits with matching waistcoats. This is a garment-level idea that can anchor a capsule.

Design notes

  • Adjust the back with a strap and buckle for fit.
  • Keep the jacket and trousers in the same cloth so they sell as a set.
  • Start with a small capsule and track sell-through.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Check sports jackets
  • Cropped suit trousers
  • Knitted ties
  • Mandarin-collar jackets
  • Matching waistcoat three-pieces
  • Pinstripe double-breasted suits

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Technical wool travel suits

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Generous 1980s double-breasted suits
  • Double-breasted overcoats
  • Linen travel suits
  • Linen waistcoats
  • Overshirt-style blazers
  • Patch pockets on soft jackets

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Double-breasted two-piece
  • Oversized lapel flower brooches
  • Pocket squares
  • Relaxed 1930s drape
  • Soft-shoulder Neapolitan jackets
  • Striped and repp ties

Actions

  • Launch a machine-washable suit.
  • Use soft, unlined construction.
  • Market comfort and care, not just looks.
  • Specify finishes (quick-dry, UV) with mills early.
  • Use certified fibres where possible.
  • Label care benefits clearly.
