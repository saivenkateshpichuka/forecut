SS27 Women's Assortment and Pricing Strategy

Plan SS27 for low growth and high costs: fewer, more flexible styles built as separates, tighter depth on fashion pieces, full price on what is new, and deliveries timed to heat and events.

Spring/Summer 2027

The market

The IMF expects global growth of 3.4% in 2027 and inflation of 3.9%, but fashion is expected to stay in low single digits[1]. Freight, energy and financing costs remain high, and excess stock both ties up cash and leads to markdowns: sales up 4% with inventory up 10% is a weaker position than steady stock[1]. Mid-market brands face promotional pressure from luxury and value players[1]. Resale is expected to grow two to three times faster than first-hand fashion in 2027[2].

Weather matters for timing: UK data show that a 1°C warmer May brings clothing sales forward from June[1].

Assortment

Tier Share of options (Forecut guide) What goes in it
Core About half Straight skirts, tailored trousers, shirts, rib knits, white and black
Seasonal About a third Midi and layered skirts, waisted jackets, satin, lilac and violet, florals
Statement The rest Gold, sheer suits, bubble skirts, beaded and feathered pieces
  • Build the range as separates in shared colours, so each piece sells with two or three others.
  • Keep statement pieces to short runs with a planned exit.
  • Cut loose trousers, chinos and mini dresses compared with SS26.

Pricing

  • Full price on what is new: rising pieces (skirts, waisted jackets, satin) are what customers come in for.
  • Value on what customers know: peaking pieces (lingerie lace, crochet) carry promotions.
  • Premium for craft: hand-applied petals, beading and silk duchesse justify higher prices; label the craft.
  • Sets: price skirt suits and denim sets to reward buying both pieces.

Timing

  • Deliver core separates early (February and March).
  • Hold sheer and single-layer pieces for the first heat, and be ready to bring them forward if spring is warm.
  • Time partywear and statement pieces for the wedding and event season.

Design implications

  • Design fewer styles with more combinations.
  • Use shared fabrics across skirts and jackets to simplify buying and reduce minimums.

Commercial implications

  • Track the sales-to-inventory ratio by category every week and chase winners rather than buying deep.
  • In the EU, plan for the unsold-goods destruction ban: buy shallower and keep stock that can be reworked or resold.
  • Consider a resale or take-back offer for premium pieces.
