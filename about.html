About

Forecasting fashion, one detail at a time.

Forecut is a global, detail-level fashion trend forecasting platform. We help designers, brands and manufacturers in any country decide which details to develop next, when, and for which market.

Why Forecut exists

Most trend reports describe a mood. The people who make clothes need specifics: which collar, which trim, which fabric, and when it will peak. And every market sells on its own calendar and climate: a trend lands in Sydney six months after Paris, sells lighter in Lagos and Jakarta, and peaks around Diwali, Ramadan, Lunar New Year or Black Friday.

Forecut brings both together. We track garments, footwear, fabrics, embroidery, trims and colours down to individual construction details, mark each one as emerging, rising, peaking or fading across the international seasons (Spring/Summer, Pre-Fall, Autumn/Winter and Resort) through 2028, and read each season for six regions.

What we offer

  • Global seasons: detail-level reports from AW26 to AW28, plus Resort and Pre-Fall, with themes and colours of the year.
  • Regional forecasts: Asia, Europe, North America, South America, Africa and Oceania, with local timing, climate, market preferences and fashion weeks.
  • India market forecast: ethnic and western wear, handloom, embroidery and trims around the Indian festive calendar, as part of the Asia region.
  • Practical detail cards: design notes, fabric and trim guidance, and downloadable cards for teams.
  • Monthly updates: forecasts are reviewed and refreshed every month.

How we work

We review public runway coverage, trade and retail reporting, street style and search interest, then use AI-assisted analysis to identify and track specific details. Every forecast is curated and written in our own words. Brands and designers are named only where public sources report them.

Editorial standards

  • Public sources only. We use fashion week schedules and reviews, trade press, company announcements and fair reports, and link to them on region pages, reports and articles.
  • Our own words. Every forecast and article is written by Forecut. We do not copy paid forecasting services.
  • Clear labels. Each season is marked as observed, forecast or projection, and every detail carries one of four stages: emerging, rising, peaking or fading.
  • Nothing quietly removed. When a trend fades we mark it as fading instead of deleting it, so the full lifecycle stays visible.
  • Honest images. Most images are AI-generated illustrations made for Forecut; real photographs are free-licensed and credited.
  • Corrections. If you spot an error, write to us and we will correct it.

Who uses Forecut

Fashion designers, D2C and retail brands, manufacturers and exporters, sourcing and merchandising teams, 3D and digital product teams, and fashion students and educators. Most users work across more than one market, which is why Forecut reads every season for six regions and their key markets.

Where we are

Forecut is an independent, global platform, made in India. It is currently in an early-access testing phase, with full access available at no cost. Your feedback directly shapes the launch.

How to use Forecut in a range plan

Start with the season that matches your next drop, not the one in stores now. If you deliver in eight to ten months, open the next-season forecast and filter by your department. Shortlist details marked rising: they are spreading but not yet everywhere, so they still feel new when your range lands. Use peaking details in proven core styles, and treat emerging details as small tests, for example one colourway or one capsule. Then open your region page and check timing: a southern-hemisphere store sells the same idea about six months later, and a hot-climate market needs lighter fabric and shorter sleeves.

Reading one trend in different markets

The same detail rarely sells the same way everywhere. A sheer organza overlay reads as evening wear in Paris, as a modest layer over an opaque slip in Dubai or Jakarta, and as festive wear in Delhi. A wool overcoat sells from August in London but becomes a light trench in Lagos. That is why every Forecut detail sits next to six regional readings, and why region pages list local moments such as Diwali, Ramadan, Lunar New Year, Black Friday and carnival.

Common questions

Is Forecut only about India? No. India is a featured market inside the Asia region, with its own festive and summer calendar, but the core forecast covers the international seasons for Asia, Europe, North America, South America, Africa and Oceania.

How often does the forecast change? News is checked daily, new details are added weekly, and stages are reviewed monthly. Old stages are kept, so you can see how a detail moved.

Can I use the detail cards with my team? Yes. Download cards are designed to be shared in briefs, tech packs and buying meetings. Please keep the Forecut credit on them.
