The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Natural tech

Natural fibres finished to perform: quick-dry linen, UV-resistant cotton and easy-care jersey.

Why now

Customers want natural fibres without the creasing and care trouble.

Key pieces

Quick-dry, Easy-care, Natural fibres; performance-jersey shirt dresses.

Source: Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Performance ease

Designs that feel intuitive and move easily: articulated sleeves and knees, stretch and breathable fabrics for sport and commuting.

Why now

Early SS28 previews point to performance design that removes restriction.

Key pieces

Articulation, Stretch, Breathable; articulated sleeves.

Source: FashionUnited, "SS28 Preview: The modern flow of performance design". Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Structured clarity

Consumers move away from constant novelty toward fewer, better pieces: clean column shapes, clear lines and minimal trims.

Why now

Early SS28 trade intelligence describes a demand for "better" fashion rather than more fashion.

Key pieces

Column shapes, Clean fronts, Minimal trims; clean column dresses.

Source: FashionUnited, "Spring Summer 2028 Trend Intelligence: The Rise of Structured Clarity". Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Parachute-poplin tuxedo shirts

Pleated-front tuxedo shirts cut in light, crisp parachute-cotton poplin; Ralph Lauren Pre-Fall 2027 evening, 6 Nov 2026. The fabric story carries this detail, so sampling and handle are key.

Design notes

  • Use pressed pleats for tailoring and soft pleats for relaxed styles.
  • Request hand-feel swatches and test for pilling, shrinkage and colour fastness.
  • Lock fabric minimums early; specialist fabrics have longer lead times.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Band-collar shirts with long placket

Collarless shirts with an extended placket. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.
  • Use a firm fusible or interlining so the collar holds its shape after washing.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Clean minimal T-shirts

Plain, well-cut tees as a base piece. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.
  • Fit on at least two sizes before committing to bulk.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Concealed-placket shirts

Fly-front plackets that hide the buttons. A placket update freshens the front without touching the pattern block.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Interface plackets so they lie flat after laundering.
  • Match buttonhole spacing to the bust or chest line to avoid gaping.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Double-pocket camp shirts

Camp-collar shirts with two chest pockets. Pockets add function and visual interest, and are easy to vary across a range.

Design notes

  • Reinforce pocket corners with bar tacks.
  • Check pocket placement against hand position on the fit model.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Parachute-poplin tuxedo shirts
  • Band-collar shirts with long placket
  • Clean minimal T-shirts
  • Concealed-placket shirts
  • Double-pocket camp shirts
  • Henley plackets

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Hidden-zip blouses
  • Performance polo tops

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Trompe-l'oeil shirt-print tops
  • Asymmetric-collar pinstripe shirts
  • Cropped boxy shirts
  • Oversized boyfriend shirts
  • Raglan baseball tees
  • Seersucker and gingham shirting

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Epaulette shirts
  • Gilded embroidered shirting
  • Lace yoke insets
  • Lace-embroidered poplin shirts
  • Slogan T-shirts
  • Vintage poster-print tops

Actions

  • Specify finishes (quick-dry, UV) with mills early.
  • Use certified fibres where possible.
  • Label care benefits clearly.
  • Add stretch to core tailoring fabrics.
  • Introduce articulated elbows on outerwear.
  • Test breathability claims before marketing them.
