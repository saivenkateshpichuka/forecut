Overview

Rich, maximal and tactile. Big outerwear, sharp tailoring and heavy embellishment, with 1920s glamour running through evening and footwear.

The headline

What is in stores now, and what keeps selling into next season.

Build into the range

Barrel trousers, Belted field coats, Belted high waists and Boxy short-sleeve shirts are rising and keep growing into SS27.

Test in small quantities

Chambray work shirts, Contrast-collar polos, Corduroy suits and Elbow-seam jumpers are new ideas to try.

Sell through

Carpenter hammer loops, Colour-block knits and Hoodie under tailoring are at their height. Do not add depth.

Reduce or rework

Cuban camp collars, High-shine puffers and Large logo intarsia are moving out.

Inspiration

The stories behind this direction and where they were seen.

Preppy sport

School and playing-field references: rugby shirts, cricket cables, varsity jackets and oversized statement collars and cuffs.

Why now

Heritage sportswear feels familiar and gift-friendly, and collars and cuffs update basics at low cost.

Seen at

Polo Ralph Lauren: Sporty prep and timeless knitwear; Willy Chavarria: Sports prep on the runway; Statement collars and cuffs: Named a top menswear runway trend

Key pieces

Rugby stripes, Cable knits, Big collars

19-6050 TCX and 11-0103 TCX

Source: Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26; Who What Wear, AW26 trends

Power tailoring

Sharp, slick suiting in pinstripes and slate grey, with pointed shoulders, peak lapels and double-breasted fronts. Oxblood accents and polished heels finish the look.

Why now

Return-to-office dressing and a move away from very oversized blazers toward sharper fits.

Seen at

Gucci: Horsebit pumps with sharp suiting; Classic-fit suits (market): Up 44% in market adoption; Double-breasted suits (market): Up 4% year on year; pointed shoulders up 12%

Key pieces

Pinstripe, Peak lapels, Pointed shoulders

19-4110 TCX and 12-5202 TCX

Source: Who What Wear (AW26 trends); Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26

Big outerwear

Oversized, protective outerwear: shearling, big fur collars, leather bombers and sculptural cocoon coats.

Why now

Outerwear is a high-value hero category, and leather bombers are growing fast.

Seen at

Acne Studios: Fur accents on outerwear; Leather bombers (market): Up 28% year on year; Harrods menswear edit: Leather jacket as the hero piece

Key pieces

Shearling, Fur trims, Leather

18-0933 TCX and 11-0110 TCX

Source: Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26; Harrods, AW26 menswear trends; Who What Wear

Strategy

How to plan the range from this forecast.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Barrel trousers
  • Belted field coats
  • Belted high waists
  • Boxy short-sleeve shirts
  • Brushed alpaca
  • Brushed cardigan coats

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Chambray work shirts
  • Contrast-collar polos
  • Corduroy suits
  • Elbow-seam jumpers
  • Fair Isle yokes
  • Flat-front slim trousers

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Carpenter hammer loops
  • Colour-block knits
  • Hoodie under tailoring
  • Overshirt chest flap pockets
  • Overshirt-style blazers
  • Oxford button-downs

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Cuban camp collars
  • High-shine puffers
  • Large logo intarsia
  • Skinny suits
  • Skinny tapered legs

Actions for the range

  • Add white contrast collars to existing shirt blocks.
  • Build one striped rugby shirt in three colourways.
  • Use tipping on knit V-necks and cuffs.
  • Offer the pinstripe suit as separates so customers can mix sizes.
  • Use pointed shoulders on jackets, but keep trousers relaxed.
  • Add a waistcoat that works alone as a top.

Pricing and cost

Indicative retail bands in ₹ for key garments, and where cost sits. A guide for planning, not a quote.

Cost level: low to medium. Contrast collars and cuffs are a cheap update to existing blocks. Cable knits use more yarn; keep cables to the front panel in value ranges.

Markets

How each region reads AW26, and the pieces from this report that fit it best.

Asia

From temperate four-season East Asia to tropical Southeast Asia and India, and the hot, dry Gulf. One collection rarely fits every market: weight, sleeve length and coverage change by country.

Best fit: Overshirt jackets, Leather overshirts, Open Johnny-collar knit polos, Striped rugby shirts

Europe

Temperate four-season climate: cold, wet winters in the north, hot dry summers around the Mediterranean.

Best fit: Utility trench details, Double-face wool overcoats, Raglan-sleeve overcoats, Mac coats

North America

From cold Canadian winters to hot, humid southern US summers and Mexico's warm highlands and coasts.

Best fit: Utility trench details, Technical parkas, Waxed cotton jackets, Sherpa-lined trucker jackets

South America

Tropical in the north and Brazil's coast; temperate and four-season in Argentina, Chile and southern Brazil. Seasons are opposite to Europe.

Best fit: Waxed cotton jackets, Printed resort shirts, Grandad collar linen shirts, Seersucker suits

Africa

Mostly hot: tropical West and Central Africa, highland East Africa, Mediterranean north, and a temperate, southern-hemisphere South Africa.

Best fit: Overshirt jackets, Leather overshirts, Striped rugby shirts, Velvet shirts

Oceania

Southern-hemisphere seasons: summer Dec–Feb, winter Jun–Aug. Mostly temperate, tropical in the north of Australia.

Best fit: Utility trench details, Printed resort shirts, Grandad collar linen shirts, Linen waistcoats
