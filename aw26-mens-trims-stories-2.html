Design notes

How to use the trims.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Double-breasted overcoats

Six-button double-breasted overcoats. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Space buttons evenly and balance the wrap width.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Fabric and trims

Tropical wool, wool-viscose suiting or linen blends, with half-canvas fronts and horn buttons. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on suits, blazers, waistcoats and smart trousers. Works in menswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Double-breasted two-piece

DB suits with wide lapels. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Balance lapel width with shoulder width and garment length.
  • Space buttons evenly and balance the wrap width.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Fabric and trims

Tropical wool, wool-viscose suiting or linen blends, with half-canvas fronts and horn buttons.

Where to use it

Best on suits, blazers, waistcoats and smart trousers. Works in menswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Tailoring, peaking

Wide-lapel suits

Broader lapels on two-piece suits. Lapel shape sets the attitude of a jacket before anything else is noticed.

Design notes

  • Balance lapel width with shoulder width and garment length.
  • Keep the jacket and trousers in the same cloth so they sell as a set.
  • Keep the roll line soft; pad-stitch or use a light canvas on premium styles.
