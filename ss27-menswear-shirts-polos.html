Shirts & Polos

Build around Boxy short-sleeve shirts, Chambray work shirts and Check shackets. The waist returns and handcraft moves into everyday clothes. Shapes get defined again, sneakers step back for elegant shoes, and colour comes back in full: purple, true red and Klein blue over sun-faded whites and sand.

The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

In motion

Long fringe that moves with every step, and bold head-to-toe stripes, from rainbow to tonal.

Why now

Movement and stripes photograph well for video-led social media.

Seen at

  • Luar, Kim Shui, Ulla Johnson, Heirlome, Monse: Long, figure-covering fringe
  • DVF: Rainbow stripes with long shorts
  • Proenza Schouler, Tommy Hilfiger: Head-to-toe stripes

Key pieces

Fringe, Stripes, Swing; fringed hems.

Source: The Zoe Report, NYFW SS27 trends

Elegant ease

Dressing gets refined again. The tie returns, shirts are tucked in, trousers are pleated and slightly puffed, and elegant mules and pointed flats replace sneakers.

Why now

Fabric forecasters say street style and sneakers are giving way to elegance.

Seen at

  • Loewe: The return of the tie
  • Munich Fabric Start forecasters: "Everything is becoming elegant"
  • SS27 menswear: Slimmer ties, pleated and puffed trousers

Key pieces

Slim ties, Mules, Pointed flats; tucked shirts with slim ties.

Source: FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage and SS27 trend report

Handcraft

Lace flowers, embroidery and smocking move onto crisp, everyday shirting. Craft techniques such as wrapping, gathering, pleating and openwork feel personal and slow.

Why now

Lace is named a growth category, and craft adds perceived value to simple poplin and knits.

Seen at

  • Chloé: Embroidery and waxed jackets
  • Gabriela Hearst: Mirrorwork inspired by Afghan craftsmanship
  • Lace (category): Forecasters call it a growth category

Key pieces

Lace, Smocking, Embroidery; lace-embroidered poplin shirts.

Source: FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage and SS27 trend report

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Boxy short-sleeve shirts

Wide, boxy short-sleeve shirts. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Grade extra ease carefully so smaller sizes do not swamp.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.
  • Fit on at least two sizes before committing to bulk.

Fabric and trims

Oxford cotton, poplin, linen blends, piqué or fine-gauge knit, with mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Chambray work shirts

Chambray shirts with two flap pockets. The fabric story carries this detail, so sampling and handle are key.

Design notes

  • Keep utility pockets flat and tidy for city wear; avoid too much bulk.
  • Request hand-feel swatches and test for pilling, shrinkage and colour fastness.
  • Lock fabric minimums early; specialist fabrics have longer lead times.

Fabric and trims

Cotton ripstop, gabardine or nylon twill with snap or Velcro closures.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Check shackets

Heavy check shirt-jackets. This is a garment-level idea that can anchor a capsule.

Design notes

  • Match checks at centre front, pockets and side seams; plan for the extra fabric.
  • Start with a small capsule and track sell-through.
  • Build two price tiers of the idea.

Fabric and trims

Yarn-dyed wool or brushed cotton checks; matching adds about 10–15% fabric.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Cuban camp collars

Camp collars fading for winter. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.
  • Use a firm fusible or interlining so the collar holds its shape after washing.

Fabric and trims

Oxford cotton, poplin, linen blends, piqué or fine-gauge knit, with mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Embroidered pocket shirts

Small embroidery on chest pockets. Embellishment lifts price perception and suits occasion and gifting drops.

Design notes

  • Place embroidery on flat areas; use backing to avoid puckering.
  • Test embellishment for snagging and washing.
  • Keep embellishment away from friction areas like underarms.

Fabric and trims

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Geometric panel piecing

Triangular and angled panels. Panelling allows colour and fabric blocking from existing materials.

Design notes

  • Use panels to block colour from existing fabrics.
  • Check shade matching across panels in bulk.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Boxy short-sleeve shirts
  • Chambray work shirts
  • Check shackets
  • Cuban camp collars
  • Embroidered pocket shirts
  • Geometric panel piecing

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Sheer-panel check shirts
  • Band-collar shirts
  • Contrast-collar polos
  • Utility overshirts with snaps

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Contrast cuffs
  • Oxford button-downs
  • Popover shirts
  • Striped rugby shirts
  • Textured knit polos

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Overshirt chest flap pockets

Actions

  • Use fringe on hems only to control cost.
  • Offer stripes as co-ord sets.
  • Shoot in motion for e-commerce and reels.
  • Add slim ties and pocket squares as add-on sales.
  • Style shirts tucked into pleated trousers in imagery.
  • Shift footwear mix from sneakers toward mules and flats.
