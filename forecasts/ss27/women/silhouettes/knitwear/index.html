Spring/Summer 2027, next season forecast. Womenswear, detailed report

Knitwear

Build around Argyle knits, Bishop-sleeve knits and Brooch-fastened cardigans. The waist returns and handcraft moves into everyday clothes. Shapes get defined again, sneakers step back for elegant shoes, and colour comes back in full: purple, true red and Klein blue over sun-faded whites and sand.

The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Handcraft

Lace flowers, embroidery and smocking move onto crisp, everyday shirting. Craft techniques such as wrapping, gathering, pleating and openwork feel personal and slow.

Why now

Lace is named a growth category, and craft adds perceived value to simple poplin and knits.

Seen at

  • Chloé: Embroidery and waxed jackets
  • Gabriela Hearst: Mirrorwork inspired by Afghan craftsmanship
  • Lace (category): Forecasters call it a growth category

Key pieces

Lace, Smocking, Embroidery; lace-embroidered poplin shirts.

Source: FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage and SS27 trend report

In motion

Long fringe that moves with every step, and bold head-to-toe stripes, from rainbow to tonal.

Why now

Movement and stripes photograph well for video-led social media.

Seen at

  • Luar, Kim Shui, Ulla Johnson, Heirlome, Monse: Long, figure-covering fringe
  • DVF: Rainbow stripes with long shorts
  • Proenza Schouler, Tommy Hilfiger: Head-to-toe stripes

Key pieces

Fringe, Stripes, Swing; fringed hems.

Source: The Zoe Report, NYFW SS27 trends

The waist returns

After years of oversized shapes the waist is back. Peplums, basque waists, balloon and bubble shapes and belted or wrapped fronts define the body again.

Why now

Forecasters see a return to elegance and shape. A defined waist refreshes existing silhouettes without new categories.

Seen at

  • Li Edelkoort, "Craft of Black": Wraps and belts the waist tightly
  • SS27 bridal and evening: Corseted bodices, basque waists and bubble skirts
  • Double belt: Returns as a waist accessory

Key pieces

Peplum, Basque waist, Bubble hem; basque waist bodices.

Source: FashionUnited, Spring/Summer 2027 trend report

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Argyle knits

Diamond argyle patterns on knits. A pattern-led update that refreshes existing silhouettes.

Design notes

  • Match pattern at centre front and key seams.
  • Plan extra fabric for pattern matching.

Fabric and trims

Merino, lambswool, cotton or recycled wool blends in 7–12 gauge, with self-yarn or horn buttons.

Where to use it

Best on crew-neck jumpers, cardigans, knit polos and vests. Works in womenswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Bishop-sleeve knits

Full sleeves gathered into long cuffs. Sleeve volume changes the silhouette more than almost any other single detail.

Design notes

  • Cut on the bias for drape and pin-test gathers on the stand.
  • Check armhole depth and movement at fit; volume needs ease.
  • Show the sleeve clearly in flat-lay and on-body imagery.

Fabric and trims

Fluid crepe, jersey or satin.

Where to use it

Best on crew-neck jumpers, cardigans, knit polos and vests. Works in womenswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Brooch-fastened cardigans

Brooches replacing buttons. Trims carry a lot of perceived value for a small cost increase.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Use a stable button band so the cardigan does not gape.
  • Specify hardware finish and test for corrosion and nickel content.

Fabric and trims

Merino, lambswool, cotton or recycled wool blends in 7–12 gauge, with self-yarn or horn buttons. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on crew-neck jumpers, cardigans, knit polos and vests. Works in womenswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Button-shoulder knits

Buttoned tabs along the shoulder. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.
  • Keep the closure consistent across a capsule for a clear story.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Cable-front cardigans

Cables running down cardigan fronts. A pattern-led update that refreshes existing silhouettes.

Design notes

  • Place cables symmetrically from the centre front.
  • Use a stable button band so the cardigan does not gape.
  • Match pattern at centre front and key seams.

Fabric and trims

Merino or lambswool in 5–7 gauge.

Where to use it

Best on crew-neck jumpers, cardigans, knit polos and vests. Works in womenswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Contrast-tipped knits

Contrast stripes at necks and cuffs. Trims give an instant seasonal update to proven styles.

Design notes

  • Match stripes at seams and centre front.
  • Use contrast to highlight the shape; check colour fastness between shades.
  • Check trim care is compatible with the main fabric.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Argyle knits
  • Bishop-sleeve knits
  • Brooch-fastened cardigans
  • Button-shoulder knits
  • Cable-front cardigans
  • Contrast-tipped knits

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Fringed macramé tops
  • Fine-gauge zip polos
  • Half-zip knits
  • Wrap cardigans

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Chunky rib turtlenecks
  • Sheer fine-gauge layers
  • Square-neck knits

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Nothing at this stage

Actions

  • Add lace trims at cuffs and hems of core shirts.
  • Use machine smocking on bodices and cuffs.
  • Partner with a craft cluster for a premium capsule.
  • Use fringe on hems only to control cost.
  • Offer stripes as co-ord sets.
  • Shoot in motion for e-commerce and reels.
