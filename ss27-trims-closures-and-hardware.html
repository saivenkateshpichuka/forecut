The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Elegant ease

Dressing gets refined again. The tie returns, shirts are tucked in, trousers are pleated and slightly puffed, and elegant mules and pointed flats replace sneakers.

Why now

Fabric forecasters say street style and sneakers are giving way to elegance.

Seen at

  • Loewe: The return of the tie
  • Munich Fabric Start forecasters: "Everything is becoming elegant"
  • SS27 menswear: Slimmer ties, pleated and puffed trousers

Key pieces

Slim ties, Mules, Pointed flats; tucked shirts with slim ties.

Source: FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage and SS27 trend report

The waist returns

After years of oversized shapes the waist is back. Peplums, basque waists, balloon and bubble shapes and belted or wrapped fronts define the body again.

Why now

Forecasters see a return to elegance and shape. A defined waist refreshes existing silhouettes without new categories.

Seen at

  • Li Edelkoort, "Craft of Black": Wraps and belts the waist tightly
  • SS27 bridal and evening: Corseted bodices, basque waists and bubble skirts
  • Double belt: Returns as a waist accessory

Key pieces

Peplum, Basque waist, Bubble hem; basque waist bodices.

Source: FashionUnited, Spring/Summer 2027 trend report

Handcraft

Lace flowers, embroidery and smocking move onto crisp, everyday shirting. Craft techniques such as wrapping, gathering, pleating and openwork feel personal and slow.

Why now

Lace is named a growth category, and craft adds perceived value to simple poplin and knits.

Seen at

  • Chloé: Embroidery and waxed jackets
  • Gabriela Hearst: Mirrorwork inspired by Afghan craftsmanship
  • Lace (category): Forecasters call it a growth category

Key pieces

Lace, Smocking, Embroidery; lace-embroidered poplin shirts.

Source: FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage and SS27 trend report

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Coats & Jackets, rising

Asymmetric wrap closures

Off-centre wrap fronts held with a tie. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Add a hidden inner tie or snap so the wrap stays closed when worn.
  • Add silicone tape or internal stays so the garment stays in place.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend melton, double-face wool, cotton gabardine or recycled-nylon shells, with horn-effect or matte metal trims.

Where to use it

Best on wool overcoats, cropped jackets, trenches and quilted puffers. Works in womenswear coats and jackets as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Coats & Jackets, rising

Belted wrap fronts

Self-fabric tie belts replacing buttons. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Add a hidden inner tie or snap so the wrap stays closed when worn.
  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend melton, double-face wool, cotton gabardine or recycled-nylon shells, with horn-effect or matte metal trims. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on wool overcoats, cropped jackets, trenches and quilted puffers. Works in womenswear coats and jackets as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Brooch-fastened cardigans

Brooches replacing buttons. Trims carry a lot of perceived value for a small cost increase.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Use a stable button band so the cardigan does not gape.
  • Specify hardware finish and test for corrosion and nickel content.

Fabric and trims

Merino, lambswool, cotton or recycled wool blends in 7–12 gauge, with self-yarn or horn buttons. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on crew-neck jumpers, cardigans, knit polos and vests. Works in womenswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Trousers, rising

Buckle side adjusters

Adjusters replacing belt loops. Trims carry a lot of perceived value for a small cost increase.

Design notes

  • Size buckles to the strap width and test for durability.
  • Specify hardware finish and test for corrosion and nickel content.
  • Order trims early; hardware often sets the critical path.

Fabric and trims

Cotton twill, wool-blend flannel, ripstop or denim, with corozo or metal shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on chinos, pleated trousers, cargo pants and jeans. Works in menswear trousers as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Footwear, Boots, rising

Buckled ankle boots

Ankle boots with a buckled strap. Trims carry a lot of perceived value for a small cost increase.

Design notes

  • Size buckles to the strap width and test for durability.
  • Specify hardware finish and test for corrosion and nickel content.
  • Order trims early; hardware often sets the critical path.

Fabric and trims

Full-grain or suede leather, elastic gussets or YKK zips, rubber commando or leather soles.

Where to use it

Best on ankle boots, knee-highs, Chelseas and lace-up boots. Use it as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Button-front midi skirts

Buttons running down the front of the skirt. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Use engineered mesh or knit for breathability.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend suiting, cotton twill, crepe or satin, with hook-and-bar closures and concealed zips. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on wide-leg trousers, tailored pants, midi and mini skirts. Works in womenswear trousers and skirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Asymmetric wrap closures
  • Belted wrap fronts
  • Brooch-fastened cardigans
  • Buckle side adjusters
  • Buckled ankle boots
  • Button-front midi skirts

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Brooch-and-chain stilettos
  • Fine-gauge zip polos
  • Half-zip knits
  • Utility overshirts with snaps

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Long exposed button fly

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Nothing at this stage

Actions

  • Add slim ties and pocket squares as add-on sales.
  • Style shirts tucked into pleated trousers in imagery.
  • Shift footwear mix from sneakers toward mules and flats.
  • Add a peplum version of your best-selling blouse.
  • Cut a basque-waist seam into a core dress block.
  • Sell double belts as an add-on accessory.
