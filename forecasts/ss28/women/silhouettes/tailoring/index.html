The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Commuter tailoring

Suits built for city days: stretch suiting, soft shoulders, concealed buttons and easy-care fabrics.

Why now

Hybrid working needs tailoring that is comfortable on a commute and smart at a desk.

Key pieces

Stretch suits, Concealed buttons, Soft shoulders; stretch commuter suiting.

Source: Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Structured clarity

Consumers move away from constant novelty toward fewer, better pieces: clean column shapes, clear lines and minimal trims.

Why now

Early SS28 trade intelligence describes a demand for "better" fashion rather than more fashion.

Key pieces

Column shapes, Clean fronts, Minimal trims; clean column dresses.

Source: FashionUnited, "Spring Summer 2028 Trend Intelligence: The Rise of Structured Clarity". Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Performance ease

Designs that feel intuitive and move easily: articulated sleeves and knees, stretch and breathable fabrics for sport and commuting.

Why now

Early SS28 previews point to performance design that removes restriction.

Key pieces

Articulation, Stretch, Breathable; articulated sleeves.

Source: FashionUnited, "SS28 Preview: The modern flow of performance design". Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Armour-structured vests

Structured tailored vests with pronounced shoulders and a protective, armour-like shape over fluid dresses; De Goeye 10th-anniversary show, Brazil (29 Oct 2026). A focused detail update for tailoring.

Design notes

  • Adjust the back with a strap and buckle for fit.

Fabric and trims

Tropical wool, wool-viscose suiting or linen blends, with half-canvas fronts and horn buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blazers, waistcoats, suit trousers and co-ord sets. Works in womenswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Belted suit jackets

Wrapped suit jackets held with a belt. Waist treatment controls the silhouette and how the garment flatters.

Design notes

  • Add a hidden inner tie or snap so the wrap stays closed when worn.
  • Keep the jacket and trousers in the same cloth so they sell as a set.
  • Mark waist position on the block and check on several body shapes.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Check tailoring

Prince of Wales and windowpane checks. The fabric story carries this detail, so sampling and handle are key.

Design notes

  • Match checks at centre front, pockets and side seams; plan for the extra fabric.
  • Request hand-feel swatches and test for pilling, shrinkage and colour fastness.
  • Lock fabric minimums early; specialist fabrics have longer lead times.

Fabric and trims

Yarn-dyed wool or brushed cotton checks; matching adds about 10–15% fabric.

Where to use it

Best on blazers, waistcoats, suit trousers and co-ord sets. Works in womenswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Collarless double-breasted jackets

Double-breasted fronts without lapels. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Balance lapel width with shoulder width and garment length.
  • Space buttons evenly and balance the wrap width.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Collarless tailored jackets

Jackets with clean V-necklines and no lapels. Changing the neckline is one of the cheapest ways to update a proven block.

Design notes

  • Balance lapel width with shoulder width and garment length.
  • Check neck opening against head circumference on pull-on styles.
  • Stay-stitch or tape the neckline so it does not stretch out.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Flap-pocket blazers

Oversized flap pockets on blazers. Pockets add function and visual interest, and are easy to vary across a range.

Design notes

  • Keep utility pockets flat and tidy for city wear; avoid too much bulk.
  • Grade extra ease carefully so smaller sizes do not swamp.
  • Reinforce pocket corners with bar tacks.

Fabric and trims

Cotton ripstop, gabardine or nylon twill with snap or Velcro closures.

Where to use it

Best on blazers, waistcoats, suit trousers and co-ord sets. Works in womenswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Armour-structured vests
  • Belted suit jackets
  • Check tailoring
  • Collarless double-breasted jackets
  • Collarless tailored jackets
  • Flap-pocket blazers

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Concealed-button blazers
  • Technical wool suiting

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Belted trench blazers
  • Cinched one-button jackets
  • Linen and seersucker suiting
  • Longline blazers
  • Pinstripe waistcoats
  • Satin tailored blazers

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Black tailoring with fuchsia accents
  • Tone-on-tone embroidered tuxedos
  • Bermuda suit shorts
  • Cropped blazers
  • Cummerbunds over suits
  • Double-breasted jackets

Actions

  • Launch a machine-washable suit.
  • Use soft, unlined construction.
  • Market comfort and care, not just looks.
  • Edit ranges down and deepen buys on proven fits.
  • Upgrade fabrics on core styles.
  • Tell a "made to last" story.
