The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Power tailoring

Sharp, slick suiting in pinstripes and slate grey, with pointed shoulders, peak lapels and double-breasted fronts. Oxblood accents and polished heels finish the look.

Why now

Return-to-office dressing and a move away from very oversized blazers toward sharper fits.

Seen at

  • Gucci: Horsebit pumps with sharp suiting
  • Classic-fit suits (market): Up 44% in market adoption
  • Double-breasted suits (market): Up 4% year on year; pointed shoulders up 12%

Key pieces

Pinstripe, Peak lapels, Pointed shoulders; sharp pointed shoulders.

Source: Who What Wear (AW26 trends); Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26

Preppy sport

School and playing-field references: rugby shirts, cricket cables, varsity jackets and oversized statement collars and cuffs.

Why now

Heritage sportswear feels familiar and gift-friendly, and collars and cuffs update basics at low cost.

Seen at

  • Polo Ralph Lauren: Sporty prep and timeless knitwear
  • Willy Chavarria: Sports prep on the runway
  • Statement collars and cuffs: Named a top menswear runway trend

Key pieces

Rugby stripes, Cable knits, Big collars; rugby stripes, short plackets.

Source: Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26; Who What Wear, AW26 trends

Roaring Twenties

1920s flapper glamour returns. Drop waists, swinging fringe, beading and long pearls lead evening, and the same codes cross into daywear through T-bar heels, velvet and pleated skirts.

Why now

Party-season demand meets nostalgia for glamour. Drop waists are easier to wear than bodycon, which helps them sell beyond evening.

Seen at

  • Chanel: Fringed silks and beaded sequins
  • Fendi: Drop-waist dresses and 1920s evening codes
  • Rabanne: Chainmail and sequin minis

Key pieces

Drop waist, Fringe, T-bar heels; drop waist with pleated skirt.

Source: Who What Wear, Autumn/Winter 2026 trends

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Denim, rising

Barrel leg with curved outseam

Curved seams shaping the leg. Seam placement adds structure and visual lines without extra trims.

Design notes

  • Shape the volume through curved side seams and darts; check on several body types.
  • Use topstitching to make seam lines visible.
  • Check seam strength at stress points.

Fabric and trims

11–14 oz cotton or cotton-lyocell denim, with copper or antique-brass rivets and shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on jeans, denim jackets, skirts and shirts. Works in womenswear denim as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Cuffed wide trousers

Wide legs finished with deep turn-ups. The hem finish changes the mood of a piece with minimal pattern work.

Design notes

  • Use a fabric with enough weight and drape so the leg falls cleanly.
  • Test hem finish after washing for twisting or fraying.
  • Keep hem treatment consistent within a capsule.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend suiting, cotton twill, crepe or satin, with hook-and-bar closures and concealed zips.

Where to use it

Best on wide-leg trousers, tailored pants, midi and mini skirts. Works in womenswear trousers and skirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Trousers, rising

Deep turn-ups

Deep cuffed hems. The hem finish changes the mood of a piece with minimal pattern work.

Design notes

  • Test hem finish after washing for twisting or fraying.
  • Keep hem treatment consistent within a capsule.

Fabric and trims

Cotton twill, wool-blend flannel, ripstop or denim, with corozo or metal shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on chinos, pleated trousers, cargo pants and jeans. Works in menswear trousers as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Double front pleats, sharp creases

Pressed creases on wide trousers. Pleats add volume and movement while keeping a tailored finish.

Design notes

  • Use pressed pleats for tailoring and soft pleats for relaxed styles.
  • Press and tack pleats so they hold through wear.
  • Balance pleat depth with hip ease.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend suiting, cotton twill, crepe or satin, with hook-and-bar closures and concealed zips.

Where to use it

Best on wide-leg trousers, tailored pants, midi and mini skirts. Works in womenswear trousers and skirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Dresses, rising

Moody tiered ruffle maxis

Boho tiers in deep, dark colours. The hem finish changes the mood of a piece with minimal pattern work.

Design notes

  • Cut ruffles on the bias or curve for a soft drape; keep volume controlled.
  • Increase the gather ratio for each tier for balanced volume.
  • Test hem finish after washing for twisting or fraying.

Fabric and trims

Viscose crepe, cotton poplin, satin-back crepe or jersey, with self-covered buttons and invisible zips.

Where to use it

Best on midi day dresses, shirt dresses, slip dresses and occasion styles. Works in womenswear dresses as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Pleated tartan minis

Kilt-style pleated minis. Pleats add volume and movement while keeping a tailored finish.

Design notes

  • Match checks at centre front, pockets and side seams; plan for the extra fabric.
  • Use pressed pleats for tailoring and soft pleats for relaxed styles.
  • Press and tack pleats so they hold through wear.

Fabric and trims

Yarn-dyed wool or brushed cotton checks; matching adds about 10–15% fabric.

Where to use it

Best on wide-leg trousers, tailored pants, midi and mini skirts. Works in womenswear trousers and skirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Barrel leg with curved outseam
  • Cuffed wide trousers
  • Deep turn-ups
  • Double front pleats, sharp creases
  • Moody tiered ruffle maxis
  • Pleated tartan minis

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Drawcord-hem cropped fleece jackets
  • Bubble hems
  • Corset-seamed dresses
  • Cuffed jean hems
  • Knit fringe at hems
  • Lace-trimmed hems

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Fringed hems
  • Western stitching

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Raw-edge blanket coats
  • Visible corset boning

Actions

  • Offer the pinstripe suit as separates so customers can mix sizes.
  • Use pointed shoulders on jackets, but keep trousers relaxed.
  • Add a waistcoat that works alone as a top.
  • Add white contrast collars to existing shirt blocks.
  • Build one striped rugby shirt in three colourways.
  • Use tipping on knit V-necks and cuffs.
