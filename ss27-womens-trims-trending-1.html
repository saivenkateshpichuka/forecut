Design notes

How to use the trims.

Womenswear, Coats & Jackets, rising

Asymmetric wrap closures

Off-centre wrap fronts held with a tie. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Add a hidden inner tie or snap so the wrap stays closed when worn.
  • Add silicone tape or internal stays so the garment stays in place.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend melton, double-face wool, cotton gabardine or recycled-nylon shells, with horn-effect or matte metal trims.

Where to use it

Best on wool overcoats, cropped jackets, trenches and quilted puffers. Works in womenswear coats and jackets as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Backless apron tops

Halter tops that tie at the neck and leave the back open. Changing the neckline is one of the cheapest ways to update a proven block.

Design notes

  • Add silicone tape or internal stays so the garment stays in place.
  • Check neck opening against head circumference on pull-on styles.
  • Stay-stitch or tape the neckline so it does not stretch out.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Band-collar shirts with long placket

Collarless shirts with an extended placket. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.
  • Use a firm fusible or interlining so the collar holds its shape after washing.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Denim, rising

Barrel leg with curved outseam

Curved seams shaping the leg. Seam placement adds structure and visual lines without extra trims.

Design notes

  • Shape the volume through curved side seams and darts; check on several body types.
  • Use topstitching to make seam lines visible.
  • Check seam strength at stress points.

Fabric and trims

11–14 oz cotton or cotton-lyocell denim, with copper or antique-brass rivets and shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on jeans, denim jackets, skirts and shirts. Works in womenswear denim as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy

What to build, test, sell through and drop.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Asymmetric wrap closures
  • Backless apron tops
  • Band-collar shirts with long placket
  • Barrel leg with curved outseam
  • Barrel-leg cargo jeans
  • Belted safari jackets

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Nothing at this stage

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Cargo midi skirts
  • Chunky rib turtlenecks
  • Contrast collars and cuffs
  • Contrast-piped edges
  • Floppy oversized collars
  • Lace-edged bias slips

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Nothing at this stage
