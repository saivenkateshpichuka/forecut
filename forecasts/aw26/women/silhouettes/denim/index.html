The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Big outerwear

Oversized, protective outerwear: shearling, big fur collars, leather bombers and sculptural cocoon coats.

Why now

Outerwear is a high-value hero category, and leather bombers are growing fast.

Seen at

  • Acne Studios: Fur accents on outerwear
  • Leather bombers (market): Up 28% year on year
  • Harrods menswear edit: Leather jacket as the hero piece

Key pieces

Shearling, Fur trims, Leather; fur and shearling collars.

Source: Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26; Harrods, AW26 menswear trends; Who What Wear

Roaring Twenties

1920s flapper glamour returns. Drop waists, swinging fringe, beading and long pearls lead evening, and the same codes cross into daywear through T-bar heels, velvet and pleated skirts.

Why now

Party-season demand meets nostalgia for glamour. Drop waists are easier to wear than bodycon, which helps them sell beyond evening.

Seen at

  • Chanel: Fringed silks and beaded sequins
  • Fendi: Drop-waist dresses and 1920s evening codes
  • Rabanne: Chainmail and sequin minis

Key pieces

Drop waist, Fringe, T-bar heels; drop waist with pleated skirt.

Source: Who What Wear, Autumn/Winter 2026 trends

Fully loaded

Embellishment moves from evening onto everyday pieces: sequinned jeans, beaded knits, feathers and sheer layers over heavier fabrics.

Why now

Social media rewards texture and shine, and embellishment makes basics feel special.

Key pieces

Sequins, Beadwork, Sheer layers; sequin and chainmail minis.

Source: Who What Wear, AW26 trends (the source describes the trend without naming brands)

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Denim, rising

Low-rise bootcut jeans

Low-slung jeans, slim through the thigh with a slight flare below the knee, as in Madhappy x Lucky Brand (Oct 2026). This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Approve wash and finish on bulk fabric; set clear shade bands.
  • Use a fabric with enough weight and drape so the leg falls cleanly.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.

Fabric and trims

11–14 oz cotton or cotton-lyocell denim, with copper or antique-brass rivets and shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on jeans, denim jackets, skirts and shirts. Works in womenswear denim as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Denim, rising

Barrel leg with curved outseam

Curved seams shaping the leg. Seam placement adds structure and visual lines without extra trims.

Design notes

  • Shape the volume through curved side seams and darts; check on several body types.
  • Use topstitching to make seam lines visible.
  • Check seam strength at stress points.

Fabric and trims

11–14 oz cotton or cotton-lyocell denim, with copper or antique-brass rivets and shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on jeans, denim jackets, skirts and shirts. Works in womenswear denim as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Denim, rising

Barrel-leg cargo jeans

Curved legs with cargo pockets. Pockets add function and visual interest, and are easy to vary across a range.

Design notes

  • Approve wash and finish on bulk fabric; set clear shade bands.
  • Shape the volume through curved side seams and darts; check on several body types.
  • Reinforce pocket corners with bar tacks.

Fabric and trims

Cotton ripstop, gabardine or nylon twill with snap or Velcro closures.

Where to use it

Best on jeans, denim jackets, skirts and shirts. Works in womenswear denim as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Denim, rising

Button-fly denim shorts

Short jeans with exposed button flies. Length shifts are simple to apply across existing blocks.

Design notes

  • Approve wash and finish on bulk fabric; set clear shade bands.
  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Update length on existing blocks rather than new patterns.

Fabric and trims

11–14 oz cotton or cotton-lyocell denim, with copper or antique-brass rivets and shank buttons. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on jeans, denim jackets, skirts and shirts. Works in womenswear denim as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Denim, rising

Carpenter jeans

Hammer loops and utility pockets. Pockets add function and visual interest, and are easy to vary across a range.

Design notes

  • Approve wash and finish on bulk fabric; set clear shade bands.
  • Keep utility pockets flat and tidy for city wear; avoid too much bulk.
  • Reinforce pocket corners with bar tacks.

Fabric and trims

Cotton ripstop, gabardine or nylon twill with snap or Velcro closures.

Where to use it

Best on jeans, denim jackets, skirts and shirts. Works in womenswear denim as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Denim, rising

Dark rinse, clean finish

Deep indigo with minimal fading. Wash and finish decide how fresh the denim looks on shelf.

Design notes

  • Approve wash and finish on bulk fabric; set clear shade bands.
  • Approve wash standards on bulk fabric, not only on samples.
  • Choose lower-impact wash methods such as laser and ozone.

Fabric and trims

11–14 oz cotton or cotton-lyocell denim, with copper or antique-brass rivets and shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on jeans, denim jackets, skirts and shirts. Works in womenswear denim as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Low-rise bootcut jeans
  • Barrel leg with curved outseam
  • Barrel-leg cargo jeans
  • Button-fly denim shorts
  • Carpenter jeans
  • Dark rinse, clean finish

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Black and grey denim
  • Cuffed jean hems
  • Denim shirt dresses
  • Raw-hem jeans
  • Sequinned and beaded jeans
  • Western yokes and pearl snaps

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Denim waistcoats
  • Long exposed button fly
  • Mid-rise relaxed jeans

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Heavy rips and distressing
  • Patchwork panels

Actions

  • Pick one hero coat per range and build marketing around it.
  • Offer detachable fur collars to stretch one coat into two looks.
  • Pair bombers with knitwear in outfit-led merchandising.
  • Lead evening with one drop-waist dress in two lengths.
  • Add fringe to hems and sleeves rather than the whole garment.
  • Pair with T-bar heels and long pearls in visual merchandising.
