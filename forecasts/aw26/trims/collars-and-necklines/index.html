The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Preppy sport

School and playing-field references: rugby shirts, cricket cables, varsity jackets and oversized statement collars and cuffs.

Why now

Heritage sportswear feels familiar and gift-friendly, and collars and cuffs update basics at low cost.

Seen at

  • Polo Ralph Lauren: Sporty prep and timeless knitwear
  • Willy Chavarria: Sports prep on the runway
  • Statement collars and cuffs: Named a top menswear runway trend

Key pieces

Rugby stripes, Cable knits, Big collars; rugby stripes, short plackets.

Source: Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26; Who What Wear, AW26 trends

Power tailoring

Sharp, slick suiting in pinstripes and slate grey, with pointed shoulders, peak lapels and double-breasted fronts. Oxblood accents and polished heels finish the look.

Why now

Return-to-office dressing and a move away from very oversized blazers toward sharper fits.

Seen at

  • Gucci: Horsebit pumps with sharp suiting
  • Classic-fit suits (market): Up 44% in market adoption
  • Double-breasted suits (market): Up 4% year on year; pointed shoulders up 12%

Key pieces

Pinstripe, Peak lapels, Pointed shoulders; sharp pointed shoulders.

Source: Who What Wear (AW26 trends); Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26

Big outerwear

Oversized, protective outerwear: shearling, big fur collars, leather bombers and sculptural cocoon coats.

Why now

Outerwear is a high-value hero category, and leather bombers are growing fast.

Seen at

  • Acne Studios: Fur accents on outerwear
  • Leather bombers (market): Up 28% year on year
  • Harrods menswear edit: Leather jacket as the hero piece

Key pieces

Shearling, Fur trims, Leather; fur and shearling collars.

Source: Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26; Harrods, AW26 menswear trends; Who What Wear

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Chunky shawl-collar cardigans

Oversized shawl collars on long cardigans. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Balance lapel width with shoulder width and garment length.
  • Keep the knit light enough to avoid sagging.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Wool blends in 3–5 gauge.

Where to use it

Best on crew-neck jumpers, cardigans, knit polos and vests. Works in womenswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Contrast collars and cuffs

White collars and cuffs on coloured shirting. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Use contrast to highlight the shape; check colour fastness between shades.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.
  • Use a firm fusible or interlining so the collar holds its shape after washing.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Coats & Jackets, rising

Detachable hoods

Hoods that zip or button off for two looks. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Use matte or two-way zips; check that the tape does not ripple.
  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend melton, double-face wool, cotton gabardine or recycled-nylon shells, with horn-effect or matte metal trims. Trims: yKK or equivalent metal and coil zips in matte finishes.

Where to use it

Best on wool overcoats, cropped jackets, trenches and quilted puffers. Works in womenswear coats and jackets as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Floppy oversized collars

Wide soft collars borrowed from school uniforms. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Keep the collar flat and well-interfaced so it does not curl.
  • Grade extra ease carefully so smaller sizes do not swamp.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Outerwear, rising

Fur and shearling collars

Fur accents on bombers and coats. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Use faux fur or recycled shearling-look pile; make fur parts detachable where you can so the garment is easier to care for.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.
  • Use a firm fusible or interlining so the collar holds its shape after washing.

Fabric and trims

Faux fur, recycled shearling-look pile or bonded borg, backed with a stable knit.

Where to use it

Best on overcoats, bombers, chore jackets, puffers and overshirts. Works in menswear outerwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Grandad collar linen shirts

Band collars on washed linen. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Approve wash and finish on bulk fabric; set clear shade bands.
  • Pre-wash linen to control shrinkage and soften the handle.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Linen or linen-cotton blends.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Chunky shawl-collar cardigans
  • Contrast collars and cuffs
  • Detachable hoods
  • Floppy oversized collars
  • Fur and shearling collars
  • Grandad collar linen shirts

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Off-shoulder tweed sheath dresses
  • Contrast-collar polos
  • Funnel neck with concealed zip
  • Funnel-neck puffers
  • Halter-neck dresses
  • Henley plackets

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Chunky rib turtlenecks
  • Leather-trimmed collars
  • Oxford button-downs
  • Popover shirts
  • Shawl-collar pullovers
  • Shearling-collar aviators

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Cuban camp collars

Actions

  • Add white contrast collars to existing shirt blocks.
  • Build one striped rugby shirt in three colourways.
  • Use tipping on knit V-necks and cuffs.
  • Offer the pinstripe suit as separates so customers can mix sizes.
  • Use pointed shoulders on jackets, but keep trousers relaxed.
  • Add a waistcoat that works alone as a top.
