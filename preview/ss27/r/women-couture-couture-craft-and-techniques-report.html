SS27 Women's Couture Craft and Techniques

The July 2026 haute couture shows set the craft agenda for SS27: inflated volume and shredded gazar at Balenciaga, poured and moulded materials at Dior and Schiaparelli, and fairy-tale embroidery at Chanel.

The evidence

The autumn/winter 2026-27 haute couture season, shown in Paris from 6 to 9 July 2026, included 30 houses, five of them new to the calendar[1]. The Associated Press called couture "the industry's laboratory and its loudest advertisement"[1].

  • Balenciaga (Pierpaolo Piccioli's debut): 3D body scans used to build new mannequins; leather and cashmere moulded by hand; balloon-hemmed gazar and a strapless gown made of 24,150 shreds of gazar; hooded feather cocoons[1]; dresses made entirely of goose and ostrich feathers, drop waists, veils and high collars[2].
  • Dior (Jonathan Anderson): inspired by the sculptor Lynda Benglis, whose techniques are pouring, knotting and moulding[2]; crushed pleated hats and sheer tasselled fans[1].
  • Chanel (Matthieu Blazy): suits, braids, tweeds and chains, with an embroidered vine running up heels and through seams[2].
  • Schiaparelli (Daniel Roseberry): corsets moulded into lifelike torsos and silicone shaped by hand[1].
  • Iris van Herpen: some 30,000 hand-blown glass beads on sheer tulle[1].
  • Elie Saab: velvet corseted gowns with New Look waists[1].

What it means for SS27 womenswear

Three couture ideas reach ready-to-wear in SS27:

  1. Volume from light cloth. Balenciaga's gazar and feathers connect to the bubble and layered skirts of SS27 ready-to-wear, where Dior used layered organza[3].
  2. The defined waist. Elie Saab's New Look waists and Balenciaga's drop waists sit alongside the cinched and peplum jackets of the SS27 shows[4].
  3. Embroidery that follows the garment. Chanel's vine embroidery running through seams is a technique that can be scaled down to a single embroidered line on a ready-to-wear jacket or skirt.

Timing

Couture ideas typically reach premium ready-to-wear within one to three seasons (Forecut assessment). Volume and waist ideas are already in SS27; seam-following embroidery is a candidate for AW27 and SS28.

Design implications

  • Borrow one couture technique per piece and keep the rest simple.
  • Use light cloth (organza, gazar-like polyester faille) for volume rather than petticoats.
  • Place embroidery along seams and edges to reduce stitch counts.

Commercial implications

  • Couture-inspired pieces belong at the top of the range; one or two hero pieces per delivery.
  • Use couture references in storytelling and campaigns, not in pricing claims.
