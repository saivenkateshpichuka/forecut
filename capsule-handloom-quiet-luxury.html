Why? Shoppers want pieces that feel special but can be worn again. Handloom also carries a clear sustainability story, and the government-backed khadi push keeps it visible.

What? Craft leads, ornament steps back. Designers showed handloom cotton, Chanderi, handspun khadi and tussar in loose, relaxed shapes with minimal surface work and even raw, unfinished edges.

Who's showing it?

  • AK|OK by Anamika Khanna: Handloom cotton and Chanderi, loose drapes, raw edges
  • Péro by Aneeth Arora: Handwoven everyday wear
  • Kartik Research: Handspun khadi with Rabari embroidery

Cost to make: Medium. True handloom has longer lead times and minimums. Use handloom for hero pieces and powerloom Chanderi-look or cotton-silk for value ranges.

How to build the capsule

  • Build a calm-colour handloom capsule: kurta sets, sarees and men's kurtas.
  • Swap heavy embroidery for tone-on-tone work at the neckline and cuffs.
  • Tell the weave and cluster story on the label and product page.

Range plan

Lead with three or four hero handloom fabrics and a simple, modern silhouette. Price the capsule in the mid to premium band and tell the weaver and region story on every product page.

  • Hero pieces (about 20%): the strongest expression of the story, at higher prices.
  • Core pieces (about 60%): easy-to-wear styles in the capsule colours.
  • Add-ons (about 20%): accessories and layers that increase basket size.

Selling it in different regions

In India, time the capsule to the festive or summer window it suits. For South Asian communities abroad, link it to local festival dates and weddings. For international customers new to Indian fashion, lead with easy pieces such as shirts, jackets and scarves, and explain the craft.

Common questions

Why is handloom seen as luxury? It is slower to make, carries regional craft and has a natural texture that machine-made cloth cannot copy.

Which customers buy it? Customers who value craft, natural fibres and long-lasting pieces, in India and internationally.
