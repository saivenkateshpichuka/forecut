How to plan a season with the Production Calendar

Start from the date that cannot move: the day styles must be listed online or on the shop floor. Enter it as the target launch, choose which days your team and suppliers work, and the calendar counts backward through each stage: design freeze, first sample, fit review and approval, lab dip and colour approval, fabric sourcing and order, bulk fabric receipt, stitching, finishing and quality check, then dispatch and transit. Each step has its own lead time, so a late lab dip shows up as a later start for everything that follows. Add a minimum buffer in calendar days; most small brands find that one or two weeks of slack is the difference between a calm launch and air freight.

Holidays and working days by region

  • India: Diwali, Dussehra, Eid, Holi and regional festivals such as Onam and Pongal close mills and tailoring units for several days; the calendar lists national holidays and major festivals so a schedule does not quietly assume work during Diwali week.
  • China and East Asia: Lunar New Year stops most factories for two to four weeks, and workers often return late, so plan bulk fabric and cutting to finish well before it.
  • Europe and North America: August holidays in Italy and France and the Christmas to New Year shutdown affect mills and logistics; Black Friday and Christmas are the retail deadlines most collections work back from.
  • Southern hemisphere: Australian, South African and South American brands launch summer ranges from September to November, so their production peaks while northern suppliers are busy with spring orders.

Common questions

What does an overdue step mean? The step should already have started or finished to meet the launch date. Shorten a later stage, move the launch, or add capacity.

Can I record what really happened? Yes. Enter actual finish dates and the calendar shows the slip in days, which is useful evidence when you negotiate lead times next season.

Can I share it? Export the schedule as a calendar file or CSV and send it to your factory or team; nothing is published automatically.
