Fabric news

New fibres, finishes and fair directions, summarised by Forecut with links to the original sources.

October 2026
Water resistance moves into the yarn

Unifi launched Resist2O Pro, a fluorine-free yarn technology that builds water resistance into the fibre instead of a topical finish, and Cofira, a yarn with the look of spun yarn and the performance of filament.

Source: Innovation in Textiles

September 2026
Première Vision AW27-28: "Living Matter"

The Paris fabric fair framed the AW27-28 season around materials as living matter, with directions on time and memory and an organic, irregular "wild" materiality. Its colour story favours nuanced neutrals and cool tones.

Source: Première Vision

August 2026
National Handloom Day 2026

India marked its 12th National Handloom Day on 7 August. Official figures cited include more than 35 lakh weavers and allied workers (about 72% women), 104 GI-registered handloom products and handloom exports of about ₹1,331 crore in 2025–26.

Source: PW Only IAS

May 2026
Denim for AW27-28: archives, tailoring and place

Première Vision Denim set out three directions: historical archives (velvets, satins and jacquards from upholstery), elevated core (denim codes in tailoring, trompe-l'oeil denim prints) and cultural denim (regional know-how and regenerative cotton).

Source: Première Vision

April 2026
Techtextil 2026 innovation awards

Winners included PFAS-free water-repellent finishes, bio-based printing pastes, a biodegradable and recyclable biopolyester (Mariva) and a biodegradable cellulose sewing and embroidery thread (AeoniQ Fil).

Source: Textile World

Upcoming fabrics

Material directions Forecut expects to grow over 2027–28.

PFAS-free performance

Water repellence built into yarns or plant-based finishes instead of fluorinated chemistry. Expect it in outerwear, swimwear and travel wear.

Bio-based polyesters

Polyester-like fibres made to be biodegradable or chemically recyclable, aimed at sportswear and functional apparel.

Mono-material garments

Cellulose sewing and embroidery threads that let a garment be made from one material family, making recycling easier.

Regenerative cotton denim

Denim from cotton grown with regenerative farming, increasingly certified and traced.

Printed illusions

Trompe-l'oeil prints that imitate denim, knits or other textures on lighter fabrics.

Interior textiles in fashion

Velvets, satins and jacquards borrowed from upholstery for AW27-28 jackets, skirts and eveningwear.

Irregular, "living" surfaces

Slubs, crinkles, uneven dyes and handmade textures that look grown rather than manufactured.

Handloom luxury

Khadi, Maheshwari and other handlooms positioned as quiet luxury, with provenance as part of the product.

New fabric innovations

Materials and processes changing the industry.

Lyocell and closed-loop fibres

Cellulose fibres made in processes that recover most of their solvent; smooth, strong and widely available.

Recycled polyester and nylon

Made from post-consumer bottles, fishing nets or textile waste; now common in outerwear and activewear.

Organic and regenerative cotton

Cotton grown with lower chemical inputs or farming that rebuilds soil health; certification matters.

Agri-waste fibres

Fibres from banana stems, pineapple leaves and other crop waste, increasingly used in blends and accessories.

Bio-based leather alternatives

Materials made from mycelium, plant waste or cactus as alternatives to animal and PU leather; still developing in durability and scale.

Waterless and low-impact dyeing

Digital printing, foam and CO₂-based dyeing aim to cut water and chemical use.

Textile-to-textile recycling

Old garments chemically or mechanically recycled into new fibre; a key focus for the next few years.

Weave techniques

How yarns interlace decides how a fabric looks, feels and wears.

Plain weave

One over, one under: the simplest and strongest structure. Poplin, voile, khadi, organza and linen.

Twill

Yarns step over two or more, creating diagonal lines. Durable and drapey: denim, gabardine, drill.

Satin

Long floats on the surface for a smooth, lustrous face. Satin, sateen and duchesse.

Basket weave

Two or more yarns woven together as one, a chunky, textured plain weave. Oxford cloth, hopsack.

Dobby

Small geometric patterns woven in: dots, diamonds and textured stripes.

Jacquard

Large, complex woven motifs controlled thread by thread. Brocades, damasks and Banarasi silks.

Leno

Warp yarns twist around the weft for an open, stable mesh. Light summer fabrics and gauze.

Pile

Extra yarns form loops or cut tufts on the surface. Velvet, corduroy and terry.

Knitting designs

The knit structures behind T-shirts, polos and sweaters.

Single jersey

Smooth face, loops on the back; light and stretchy. T-shirts.

Rib (1×1, 2×2)

Alternating knit and purl columns; very elastic. Cuffs, neckbands, fitted tops.

Interlock

Double-knit, smooth on both sides, stable. Premium tees and babywear.

Piqué

Textured honeycomb surface. Polo shirts.

Waffle / thermal

Grid of raised squares that trap air. Henleys, layering.

Cable

Stitches crossed over each other to form ropes. Fisherman and cricket sweaters.

Pointelle

Small eyelets arranged in patterns. Light summer knits.

Openwork / crochet look

Large open stitches for breathable, lacy knits. Summer cover-ups.

Intarsia

Blocks of colour knitted in without floats. Logos, graphic motifs.

Jacquard knit / fair isle

Multi-colour patterns with floats or backing. Winter knits.

Warp knit (tricot, raschel)

Machine knits made lengthwise; run-resistant. Linings, lace, activewear.

Fully fashioned

Panels knitted to shape instead of cut, with fashion marks at the seams. Fine-gauge knitwear.

Prints & printing techniques

From hand block printing to digital: how prints are made.

Hand block print

Carved wooden blocks stamped by hand: Bagru, Sanganeri and Ajrakh are famous Indian traditions.

Dabu (mud resist)

A mud-paste resist applied before dyeing, giving soft cracked outlines; from Rajasthan.

Kalamkari

Pen-drawn or block-printed designs coloured with natural dyes; from Andhra Pradesh.

Batik

Wax resist applied by hand or block before dyeing, with characteristic crackle lines.

Shibori & tie-dye

Fabric folded, stitched, bound or clamped before dyeing; bandhani is India's fine tie-dye.

Screen & rotary print

Ink pushed through screens for crisp repeats; the workhorse of mass printing.

Digital print

Designs printed directly from a file, allowing photographic detail, short runs and less water.

Discharge print

Colour removed from dyed fabric to create lighter patterns with a soft handle.

Foil & metallic

Metallic foils or pigments bonded to fabric for festive shine.

Devoré (burnout)

Chemicals dissolve one fibre in a blend, leaving sheer patterns against velvet or satin.

Embroidery fabrics

Choosing the right base for each kind of embroidery and embellishment.

Net & tulle

Light, open and transparent. Ideal for scattered sequins, appliqué and floating 3D flowers.

Organza

Crisp and sheer. Holds structured embroidery, cut-work and petals beautifully.

Georgette & chiffon

Fluid and light. Best for lighter thread work and scattered embellishment.

Raw silk & dupion

Firm and slubby. Carries heavy zardozi and dense thread work without puckering.

Velvet

Rich pile that makes metallic embroidery glow; needs careful handling.

Chanderi

Light silk-cotton with sheen. Good for gota, zari borders and light resham.

Cotton mulmul & voile

Soft and fine. The traditional base for chikankari and shadow work.

Crepe

Matte and drapey. Suits sequin and bead work on gowns and sarees.

Linen & khadi

Textured, natural bases for kantha, Kutch work and contemporary thread embroidery.

Hand work & embroideries

Hand embroidery traditions from India and around the world.

Chikankari

Lucknow

White-on-white shadow and knot work on fine cotton.

Zardozi

Lucknow, Agra, Delhi

Raised metal-thread embroidery for bridal and couture.

Kantha

Bengal, Odisha

Running-stitch quilting and embroidery.

Phulkari

Punjab

Darning-stitch floss silk embroidery that can cover the cloth.

Kasuti

Karnataka

Counted-thread geometric embroidery, identical on both sides.

Kutch work

Gujarat

Interlaced stitches and mirror work in bright colours.

Aari

Kashmir, Gujarat and widely

Fine chain stitch made with a hooked needle.

Sozni & crewel

Kashmir

Fine needle embroidery on shawls; crewel in wool on cotton.

Gota patti

Rajasthan

Appliquéd gold and silver ribbon motifs.

Pipli appliqué

Odisha

Brightly coloured cut fabric appliqué.

Lambani

Karnataka, Telangana

Mirror, coin and pattern-darning embroidery.

Toda

Nilgiris

Red and black darning embroidery on white cotton.

Sashiko

Japan

Running-stitch reinforcement embroidery, now a global visible-mending style.

Broderie anglaise

Europe

White cut-work eyelet embroidery on cotton.

Smocking

Europe

Gathered fabric held in decorative stitches, popular on summer dresses.

Indian handlooms & textile crafts

India's great weaving and dyeing traditions.

Khadi

Hand-spun, hand-woven cotton, silk or wool. Breathable with a gentle slub.

Chanderi

Light, sheer silk-cotton from Madhya Pradesh with zari motifs.

Maheshwari

Silk-cotton from Maheshwar with reversible borders and stripes.

Banarasi

Varanasi silk brocades with rich zari jacquard motifs.

Kanjeevaram

Heavy mulberry silk from Tamil Nadu with contrast borders.

Jamdani

Fine muslin from Bengal with motifs inlaid by hand on the loom.

Paithani

Maharashtrian silk with tapestry-woven peacock and lotus borders.

Patola

Double ikat silk from Patan, Gujarat: warp and weft both resist-dyed.

Pochampally ikat

Telangana ikat with sharp geometric patterns.

Kota doria

Rajasthani cotton-silk with a fine checked grid.

Bandhani

Tie-dye from Gujarat and Rajasthan: tiny resist-tied dots.

Ajrakh

Block-printed, resist-dyed cotton from Kutch and Sindh in indigo and madder.

Kalamkari

Hand-painted or block-printed cotton from Andhra Pradesh using natural dyes.
