Design notes

How to use the trims.

Menswear, Knitwear, rising

Funnel-neck zip knits

Full zips on funnel-neck knits. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Keep the funnel high but soft at the front so it is comfortable under the chin.
  • Use matte or two-way zips; check that the tape does not ripple.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Fabric and trims

Merino, lambswool, cotton or recycled wool blends in 7–12 gauge, with self-yarn or horn buttons. Trims: yKK or equivalent metal and coil zips in matte finishes.

Where to use it

Best on crew necks, quarter-zips, cardigans and knit polos. Works in menswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Trousers, rising

Side-tab trousers

Side tabs instead of belt loops. Trims carry a lot of perceived value for a small cost increase.

Design notes

  • Specify hardware finish and test for corrosion and nickel content.
  • Order trims early; hardware often sets the critical path.

Fabric and trims

Cotton twill, wool-blend flannel, ripstop or denim, with corozo or metal shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on chinos, pleated trousers, cargo pants and jeans. Works in menswear trousers as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Snap-front western shirts

Pointed yokes and pearl snaps. Trims carry a lot of perceived value for a small cost increase.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Add stitching on the shaft and a stacked heel.
  • Specify hardware finish and test for corrosion and nickel content.

Fabric and trims

Oxford cotton, poplin, linen blends, piqué or fine-gauge knit, with mother-of-pearl effect buttons. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Utility overshirts with snaps

Snap-front overshirts with chest pockets. Trims carry a lot of perceived value for a small cost increase.

Design notes

  • Keep utility pockets flat and tidy for city wear; avoid too much bulk.
  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Specify hardware finish and test for corrosion and nickel content.

Fabric and trims

Cotton ripstop, gabardine or nylon twill with snap or Velcro closures.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.
