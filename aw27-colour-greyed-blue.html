The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Quiet structure

Clean lines: pleated wool trousers, funnel necks, concealed closures and straight shapes without decoration.

Why now

A calmer reaction to heavy embellishment, and pieces that last more than one season.

Key pieces

Pleats, Funnel necks, Clean fronts; high-waisted pleated trousers.

Source: Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Deep blue

Bright cobalt blue deepens for winter into cobalt and ink on knitwear, wool coats and leather.

Why now

Colours of the year usually carry through both seasons of their year in adapted shades.

Key pieces

Cobalt, Ink blue, Knitwear; funnel-neck knits.

Source: Based on Forecut's colour direction for the season. Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Waisted outerwear

The defined waist moves into winter outerwear: belted and double-belted coats, wrap fronts and cinched jackets.

Why now

SS27 brought the waist back; outerwear is the natural next step for autumn.

Key pieces

Belted coats, Double belts, Wrap fronts; double-belted waists.

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Generous 1980s double-breasted suits

Roomy double-breasted suits with pleated, higher-waisted trousers in a late-80s/early-90s line; the VRC's Melbourne Cup Carnival 2026 forecast names them for race-day menswear, alongside the return of the tie. A focused detail update for tailoring.

Design notes

  • Use pressed pleats for tailoring and soft pleats for relaxed styles.
  • Space buttons evenly and balance the wrap width.

Fabric and trims

Tropical wool, wool-viscose suiting or linen blends, with half-canvas fronts and horn buttons.

Where to use it

Best on suits, blazers, waistcoats and smart trousers. Works in menswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Clean straight-leg trousers

Straight legs with pressed creases. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.
  • Fit on at least two sizes before committing to bulk.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend suiting, cotton twill, crepe or satin, with hook-and-bar closures and concealed zips.

Where to use it

Best on wide-leg trousers, tailored pants, midi and mini skirts. Works in womenswear trousers and skirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Denim, rising

Clean wide-leg trouser jeans

Wide jeans cut like trousers, with creases. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Approve wash and finish on bulk fabric; set clear shade bands.
  • Use a fabric with enough weight and drape so the leg falls cleanly.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.

Fabric and trims

11–14 oz cotton or cotton-lyocell denim, with copper or antique-brass rivets and shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on jeans, denim jackets, skirts and shirts. Works in womenswear denim as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Collarless tailored jackets

Jackets with clean V-necklines and no lapels. Changing the neckline is one of the cheapest ways to update a proven block.

Design notes

  • Balance lapel width with shoulder width and garment length.
  • Check neck opening against head circumference on pull-on styles.
  • Stay-stitch or tape the neckline so it does not stretch out.

Fabric and trims

Tropical wool, wool-viscose suiting or linen blends, with half-canvas fronts and horn buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blazers, waistcoats, suit trousers and co-ord sets. Works in womenswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Dresses, rising

Column knit dresses

Long ribbed knit dresses in column shapes. The fabric story carries this detail, so sampling and handle are key.

Design notes

  • Use a rib with good recovery so it holds its shape.
  • Request hand-feel swatches and test for pilling, shrinkage and colour fastness.
  • Lock fabric minimums early; specialist fabrics have longer lead times.

Fabric and trims

Cotton-elastane or merino rib.

Where to use it

Best on midi day dresses, shirt dresses, slip dresses and occasion styles. Works in womenswear dresses as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Cropped boxy shirts

Short camp-collar shirts with straight hems. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Grade extra ease carefully so smaller sizes do not swamp.
  • Check the crop length against high-rise bottoms.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Generous 1980s double-breasted suits
  • Clean straight-leg trousers
  • Clean wide-leg trouser jeans
  • Collarless tailored jackets
  • Column knit dresses
  • Cropped boxy shirts

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Lacquered funnel-neck trenches
  • Parachute-poplin tuxedo shirts
  • Clean column dresses
  • Concealed-placket shirts
  • Fly-front placket shirts
  • Funnel-neck base layers

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Cup-bodice gold column gowns
  • Lace-up corset-back sequin gowns
  • Clean leather bombers
  • Collarless cropped jackets
  • Flat-front straight trousers
  • Photographic and art prints

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Nothing at this stage

Actions

  • Invest in fabric quality over trims.
  • Build a capsule of five clean core pieces.
  • Use concealed plackets to update shirts.
  • Lab-dip cobalt and ink shades across knit, woven and leather.
  • Use deep blue as a core neutral, not just an accent.
  • Pair with cognac and cream.
