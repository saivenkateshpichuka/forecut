Design notes

How to use the leading trims.

Menswear, Trousers, rising

Articulated-knee trousers

Shaped knees for movement. Seam placement adds structure and visual lines without extra trims.

Design notes

  • Use topstitching to make seam lines visible.
  • Check seam strength at stress points.

Fabric and trims

Cotton twill, wool-blend flannel, ripstop or denim, with corozo or metal shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on chinos, pleated trousers, cargo pants and jeans. Works in menswear trousers as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Contrast-collar polos

Polos with contrast collars. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Keep the collar flat with a firm rib or interfacing.
  • Use contrast to highlight the shape; check colour fastness between shades.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Piqué, fine-gauge knit or jersey.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Outerwear, rising

Funnel-neck puffers

Puffers with tall funnel necks. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Plan channel width with the silhouette: wider channels look bolder, narrow channels look more refined.
  • Keep the funnel high but soft at the front so it is comfortable under the chin.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Recycled nylon or matte peached shells with recycled polyester wadding.

Where to use it

Best on overcoats, bombers, chore jackets, puffers and overshirts. Works in menswear outerwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Satin side-stripe trousers

Evening trousers with satin stripes. Seam placement adds structure and visual lines without extra trims.

Design notes

  • Cut with care; pile and sheen change with direction, so lay all pieces one way.
  • Match stripes at seams and centre front.
  • Use topstitching to make seam lines visible.

Fabric and trims

Viscose velvet, satin-back crepe or washable silk-feel polyester.

Where to use it

Best on suits, blazers, waistcoats and smart trousers. Works in menswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.
