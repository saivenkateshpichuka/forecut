The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Performance ease

Designs that feel intuitive and move easily: articulated sleeves and knees, stretch and breathable fabrics for sport and commuting.

Why now

Early SS28 previews point to performance design that removes restriction.

Key pieces

Articulation, Stretch, Breathable; articulated sleeves.

Source: FashionUnited, "SS28 Preview: The modern flow of performance design". Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Natural tech

Natural fibres finished to perform: quick-dry linen, UV-resistant cotton and easy-care jersey.

Why now

Customers want natural fibres without the creasing and care trouble.

Key pieces

Quick-dry, Easy-care, Natural fibres; performance-jersey shirt dresses.

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Argyle knits

Diamond argyle patterns on knits. A pattern-led update that refreshes existing silhouettes.

Design notes

  • Match pattern at centre front and key seams.
  • Plan extra fabric for pattern matching.

Fabric and trims

Merino, lambswool, cotton or recycled wool blends in 7–12 gauge, with self-yarn or horn buttons.

Where to use it

Best on crew-neck jumpers, cardigans, knit polos and vests. Works in womenswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Bishop-sleeve knits

Full sleeves gathered into long cuffs. Sleeve volume changes the silhouette more than almost any other single detail.

Design notes

  • Cut on the bias for drape and pin-test gathers on the stand.
  • Check armhole depth and movement at fit; volume needs ease.
  • Show the sleeve clearly in flat-lay and on-body imagery.

Fabric and trims

Fluid crepe, jersey or satin.

Where to use it

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Cropped cardigans

Short cardigans ending at the waist. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Use a stable button band so the cardigan does not gape.
  • Check the crop length against high-rise bottoms.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Fair Isle yoke cardigans

Patterned yokes on button cardigans. A pattern-led update that refreshes existing silhouettes.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Limit colours per row to control float length and cost.
  • Match pattern at centre front and key seams.

Fabric and trims

Where to use it

Best on crew-neck jumpers, cardigans, knit polos and vests. Works in womenswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Fine-gauge zip polos

Collared knits with a short zip. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Use matte or two-way zips; check that the tape does not ripple.
  • Keep the collar flat with a firm rib or interfacing.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Fabric and trims

Merino, lambswool, cotton or recycled wool blends in 7–12 gauge, with self-yarn or horn buttons. Trims: yKK or equivalent metal and coil zips in matte finishes.

Where to use it

Best on crew-neck jumpers, cardigans, knit polos and vests. Works in womenswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Knitwear, rising

Funnel-neck knits

High funnel necks on fine and chunky knits. Changing the neckline is one of the cheapest ways to update a proven block.

Design notes

  • Keep the funnel high but soft at the front so it is comfortable under the chin.
  • Keep the knit light enough to avoid sagging.
  • Check neck opening against head circumference on pull-on styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Argyle knits
  • Bishop-sleeve knits
  • Cropped cardigans
  • Fair Isle yoke cardigans
  • Fine-gauge zip polos
  • Funnel-neck knits

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Seamless performance knits

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Button-shoulder knits
  • Cable-front cardigans
  • Chunky rib turtlenecks
  • Contrast-tipped knits
  • Crochet tops and cardigans
  • Openwork and pointelle knits

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Brooch-fastened cardigans
  • Elongated rib cuffs
  • Knit fringe at hems
  • Knitted polo collars
  • Shawl-collar knit coats
  • Square-neck knits

Actions

  • Add stretch to core tailoring fabrics.
  • Introduce articulated elbows on outerwear.
  • Test breathability claims before marketing them.
  • Specify finishes (quick-dry, UV) with mills early.
  • Use certified fibres where possible.
  • Label care benefits clearly.
