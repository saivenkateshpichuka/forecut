The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Functional tailoring

Tailoring that works hard: belted jackets, useful pockets, clean seams and cargo details in wool.

Why now

Continues SS28 performance thinking into winter suiting.

Key pieces

Belted jackets, Utility pockets, Clean seams; belted suit jackets.

Key pieces

Stretch wool, Water-resistant, Travel suits; technical wool suiting.

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Satin side-stripe trousers

Evening trousers with satin stripes. Seam placement adds structure and visual lines without extra trims.

Design notes

  • Cut with care; pile and sheen change with direction, so lay all pieces one way.
  • Match stripes at seams and centre front.
  • Use topstitching to make seam lines visible.

Fabric and trims

Viscose velvet, satin-back crepe or washable silk-feel polyester.

Where to use it

Best on suits, blazers, waistcoats and smart trousers. Works in menswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Single-pleat suit trousers

One forward pleat on suit trousers. Pleats add volume and movement while keeping a tailored finish.

Design notes

  • Use pressed pleats for tailoring and soft pleats for relaxed styles.
  • Keep the jacket and trousers in the same cloth so they sell as a set.
  • Press and tack pleats so they hold through wear.

Fabric and trims

Tropical wool, wool-viscose suiting or linen blends, with half-canvas fronts and horn buttons.

Where to use it

Best on suits, blazers, waistcoats and smart trousers. Works in menswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Tailoring, rising

Technical wool travel suits

Crease-resistant wool with hidden closures. The fabric story carries this detail, so sampling and handle are key.

Design notes

  • Keep the jacket and trousers in the same cloth so they sell as a set.
  • Request hand-feel swatches and test for pilling, shrinkage and colour fastness.
  • Lock fabric minimums early; specialist fabrics have longer lead times.

Fabric and trims

Menswear, Tailoring, peaking

Angrakha-overlap tunics

Tunics with an overlapping angrakha-style front worn under a bandhgala, as at Tasva by Tarun Tahiliani, Lakmē Fashion Week x FDCI, Delhi (Oct 2026); a festive idea for India and the Gulf. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Use laser finishing for consistent, lower-impact distressing.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.
  • Keep the closure consistent across a capsule for a clear story.

Menswear, Tailoring, peaking

Check sports jackets

Checked jackets worn with odd trousers. The fabric story carries this detail, so sampling and handle are key.

Design notes

  • Match checks at centre front, pockets and side seams; plan for the extra fabric.
  • Use laser finishing for consistent, lower-impact distressing.
  • Request hand-feel swatches and test for pilling, shrinkage and colour fastness.

Fabric and trims

Yarn-dyed wool or brushed cotton checks; matching adds about 10–15% fabric.

Where to use it

Best on suits, blazers, waistcoats and smart trousers. Works in menswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Tailoring, peaking

Cropped suit trousers

Suit trousers cut above the ankle. Length shifts are simple to apply across existing blocks.

Design notes

  • Keep the jacket and trousers in the same cloth so they sell as a set.
  • Check the crop length against high-rise bottoms.
  • Update length on existing blocks rather than new patterns.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Satin side-stripe trousers
  • Single-pleat suit trousers
  • Technical wool travel suits

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Quilted dinner jackets

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Angrakha-overlap tunics
  • Check sports jackets
  • Cropped suit trousers
  • Knitted ties
  • Mandarin-collar jackets
  • Matching waistcoat three-pieces

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Generous 1980s double-breasted suits
  • Corduroy suits
  • Double-breasted overcoats
  • Grey melange flannel
  • Overshirt-style blazers
  • Patch pockets on soft jackets

Actions

  • Add one useful pocket to core tailoring.
  • Belt a suit jacket.
  • Offer a utility vest as a layering piece.
  • Source technical wool samples early.
  • Test water resistance and stretch recovery.
  • Position as investment pieces.
