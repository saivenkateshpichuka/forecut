SS27 Kids' Pool Days

Summer swim and sun protection move to the centre of kidswear: UPF50+ swimwear and tees, quick-dry sets, beach towels and coordinated pool looks.

Season
Spring/Summer 2027
Audience
Kids
Category
Themes
Published
2026-11-25
Research period
Pitti Bimbo 103, Florence, 24-25 June 2026; Playtime Paris, 28-30 June 2026; analysis July to November 2026
Market
Europe-led kidswear trade fairs; premium and mid-market childrenswear, newborn to teen
Trend status
Rising

The story

Pitti Bimbo 103 took "The Pitti Pool" as its theme and called itself "the most colorful summer edition yet"[1]. Swimwear, T-shirts and accessories with built-in UV filters were prominent: Fortune Italia notes they were recently niche products and are now becoming more common, driven by family concern for health and by changing climate conditions[2]. Newer lines emphasise breathability, quick drying and comfort[2].

Exhibitors showed the idea across the range. Piccoli Principi's "Pearl & Tides" swimwear in two-pieces and swimsuits uses technical fabrics including sustainable lycra, and "each piece guarantees UV protection"; Teeter Totter's "Aegean Chromatique" offers UPF50+ swim and sportswear, backpacks and ultra-light, sand-free beach towels; Marie Raxevisky's "Beach Couture" line includes UPF 50+ certified swimwear, sarongs and skirts[3].

Colours

Aqua and soft navy with vintage pink and pastel green[3], plus red as the accent. See SS27 Kids' Season Colours.

Silhouettes

  • One-piece and two-piece swimsuits; long-sleeve swim tops.
  • Swim shorts and board shorts with matching tops.
  • Cover-ups: sarongs, skirts and terry tops.

Fabrics

UPF-rated swim fabrics including recycled or sustainable lycra[3]; quick-dry performance knits[2]; terry for cover-ups[4].

Trims and details

Flat seams, covered elastics, zip garages at the neck, and labels printed rather than sewn where skin contact is high.

Timing

In stores March to August 2027; strongest for holiday and pool months. Forecut expects UV-protective kidswear to keep growing beyond SS27.

Products and customers

  • UPF swimwear, rash guards, UV tees and hats, swim shorts, cover-ups, beach towels and bags.
  • Customers: families buying for holidays and summer camps; grandparents buying gifts.

Design implications

  • Only claim UPF ratings that have been tested to a recognised standard such as EN 13758 or AS/NZS 4399, and state the rating on the label.
  • Design swim, tee and cover-up as one coordinated set.
  • Safety: no drawstrings or long cords at the hood, neck or waist of young children's clothing, in line with EN 14682; test the attachment strength of buttons, beads, shells and other decorations, and keep small detachable parts off baby and toddler pieces.

Commercial implications

  • Build depth in swimwear and UV tees; they sell as sets.
  • UPF ratings support full price; keep them visible on product pages.
