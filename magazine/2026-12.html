Regional market · 19 November 2026

Selling SS27 in Southeast Asia: Jakarta's modest wear, Lunar New Year and Ramadan in one week

How to plan SS27 for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and the wider region: Jakarta Fashion Week 2027 trends, the February 2027 calendar and e-Conomy SEA data.

Southeast Asia does not have an autumn. Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines are hot and humid all year, with wet and dry seasons instead of four, so Forecut's Asia data treats the region as a market where SS27 weights sell for twelve months and AW27 arrives only as light layering pieces. What changes the calendar is culture, not climate. In 2027 the two biggest dressing moments land almost on top of each other, and right at the start of the SS27 delivery window.

February 2027: Lunar New Year and Ramadan in the same week

Selling SS27 in Southeast Asia: Jakarta's modest wear, Lunar New Year

Lunar New Year falls on 6 February 2027. Ramadan is expected to begin about two days later, on around 8 February, with Eid al-Fitr (Hari Raya Aidilfitri) on 9 or 10 March, subject to moon sighting, according to UAE Astronomy Centre projections reported by The National. For Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia, which celebrate both, this means red-and-gold Lunar New Year capsules and modest Raya ranges need to be in stores at the same time, in late January.

For manufacturers, that pulls production forward. Both ranges should be finished and shipped in December and early January, before factory holidays around Lunar New Year slow output in China and parts of the region.

A growing, AI-assisted online market

The 11th e-Conomy SEA report from Google, Temasek and Bain & Company (reported by TNGlobal on 21 October) expects the region's digital economy to grow 17% to $368 billion in gross merchandise value in 2026, with e-commerce up 18% to $226 billion. It found that 69% of shoppers already use AI to compare complex purchases and 75% would let AI agents find the best price and complete a purchase. Clear product data matters more when an assistant is doing the comparing: fabric content, sleeve length, lining and coverage should be stated plainly on every listing.

Regional market · 16 November 2026

Selling SS27 in North America: holiday spending, tariff costs and what US, Canada and Mexico buyers want

How to plan SS27 for the US, Canada and Mexico: the NRF holiday forecast, new research on tariff pass-through, Canadian and Buen Fin data and NYFW trends.

North America is the biggest single apparel market in Forecut's six regions, and all three of its main countries follow the northern calendar: SS27 reaches stores from February 2027. But the US, Canada and Mexico are in very different moods this autumn. US shoppers are still spending, Canadian clothing sales are growing while prices fall, and in Mexico fashion is the top category for this week's Buen Fin sales event.

United States: a $1 trillion holiday, with careful retailers

The National Retail Federation forecast on 15 October that holiday sales in November and December will grow 4.2% to $1.013 trillion, faster than last year's 3.1% rise to $972 billion. Retailers are more cautious than shoppers: NRF expects 325,000 to 375,000 seasonal hires against about 356,000 last year, citing higher gas prices, persistent inflation, higher interest rates and consumer debt.

Selling SS27 in North America: holiday spending, tariff costs and what

Tariffs are the other big question for SS27 costing. A new study by Sheng Lu of the University of Delaware (5 November), using monthly US government data from 2015 to 2025, found that tariff shocks explain only about 1.2% of the variation in US apparel import prices and about 5% of the variation in retail prices over ten months. Retail prices showed no significant rise straight after a tariff shock; a small rise appeared about three months later and soon faded. In plain terms, US retailers rarely pass tariff costs straight to shoppers, so the pressure lands on brand and supplier margins. Duty rules have changed several times since 2025, so confirm the current rate for each country of origin…

Canada: sales up, prices down, fewer stores

Statistics Canada data reported by FashionUnited and Retail Insider show sales at clothing and clothing accessories retailers of C$3.205 billion in July, up 5.5% on a year earlier. Prices are moving the other way: clothing prices were 1.1% lower year on year in August, with men's clothing down 2.3%, while clothing accessories, watches and jewellery rose 12.1%.

Retail Insider's Q3 report says store productivity now matters more than store count. Harry Rosen replaced a Bloor Street store of more than 50,000 sq ft with a 38,000 sq ft Yorkville flagship. lululemon's Canadian revenue fell 11% in its second quarter and it cut about 15% of SKUs in North American stores. UNIQLO's renovated Montreal flagship added customisation, embroidery and repair, Arc'teryx opened a Montreal store with repair and resale. For SS27, that means tighter ranges, light technical layers for a cool spring, and clear repair or resale messages.

What shoppers are spending

The market is steady rather than booming. Global enterprise platforms. Sourcing · 12 November 2026

Europe's ban on destroying unsold clothes: what it means for SS27 buys, AW26 clearance and textile EPR

The EU ban on destroying unsold clothes and shoes now applies to large firms. What it means for SS27 buys, AW26 clearance and textile EPR for anyone selling to Europe.

Since 19 July 2026, large companies in the European Union may no longer destroy unsold clothes, clothing accessories and footwear. The European Commission calls it the first product group under the new ban, set under the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR). It matters well beyond Europe: any brand, wholesaler or manufacturer that ships stock into the EU now has to plan what happens to the pieces that do not sell. With SS27 orders being finalised and AW26 heading into the January sales, this guide explains the rules and what to change in buying, production and clearance.

What the ban says

Europe's ban on destroying unsold clothes: what it means for SS27 buys

The Commission says companies must prioritise keeping products in use: selling them, including at a discount or in other markets, donating them to charities or social enterprises, or preparing them for reuse through repair, refurbishment or remanufacturing. Any destruction that is still allowed must follow the waste hierarchy, with recycling first. The rule applies to large companies from 19 July 2026 and to medium-sized companies from 19 July 2030. Small and micro businesses are exempt. Vendor guides list the covered goods by customs code: apparel and clothing accessories (CN 61, 62, 4203, 6504 and 6505) and footwear (CN 6401 to 6405).

The scale is real. The Commission cites European Environment Agency estimates that 4–9% of all textile products put on the European market are destroyed before use, or 264,000 to 594,000 tonnes a year.

Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/296 sets out the exceptions. As summarised by MyProductPassport, destruction remains possible only with documented proof in cases such as:

Companies relying on an exception must keep evidence, such as documents or test results, for five years, and publish annual reports on what they discarded and why. National authorities enforce the rule and can impose fines.

Menswear · 19 October 2026

Men's wedding wear 2026–27: bandhgalas, sherwanis and quieter embroidery

What grooms and guests will wear this wedding season: slimmer sherwanis, velvet and tonal bandhgalas, Nehru jackets, deep jewel tones and rupee price bands.

For menswear, the Indian wedding season is the biggest selling window of the year. The Confederation of All India Traders (CAIT) estimated that the 2025 season, from 1 November to 14 December, would see about 46 lakh weddings and ₹6.5 lakh crore of spending, with apparel and sarees taking around a tenth of it. Most of those weddings have several functions, and men now dress for each one rather than wearing a single suit throughout.

This edit brings together what designers showed at India Couture Week 2026, recent groomwear guides from Indian retailers, and Forecut's own detail tracking for Festive 26 (October 2026 to February 2027). It is written for designers, brands and manufacturers planning ranges for grooms, close family and guests.

The big idea: polish over ornament

Men's wedding wear 2026–27: bandhgalas, sherwanis and quieter embroide

The clearest shift is away from head-to-toe embellishment. Aza Fashions' September 2026 groomwear outlook describes slimmer sherwanis with clean cuts and structured shoulders, closed-collar bandhgalas and restrained, tone-on-tone embroidery. Esquire India's August 2026 review of India Couture Week pointed to fluid tailoring, dramatic drapes and unexpected layering, with Anamika Khanna showing a clean ivory set with gold work and Kunal Rawal focusing on layering. Tarun Tahiliani and JJ Valaya kept the classic, regal look alive for those who still want it.

For most brands, the commercial message is simple: the fabric, the fit and the finish now do more of the work than the embroidery. That suits manufacturers too, because it shifts cost from hand-work hours to pattern quality and cloth.

Bandhgalas lead the evening

The bandhgala, or Jodhpuri, is the strongest single garment for receptions and evening functions. Forecut tracks two versions rising into this season. Tone-on-tone bandhgalas use embroidery in the same colour as the cloth, so they look rich without looking loud. Velvet bandhgalas in bottle green, navy and burgundy are the winter-wedding piece, and a collar brooch or statement button is the easy finishing detail.

Sherwanis get slimmer and lighter in colour

The sherwani remains the groom's main look, but the shape is cleaner and closer to the body. For day weddings, ivory and pastel sherwanis in ivory, mint and powder shades are at their peak. Ivory also stayed on the India Couture Week runway, now joined by deeper reds and blush pinks.
