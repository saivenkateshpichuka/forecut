Design notes

How to use the saree layout designer

A saree is planned along its length. From the inner end it usually runs: the blouse piece (if woven in, about 80 cm to 1 m), the body that is pleated and tucked, and the pallu, the decorated end that falls over the shoulder, typically 70 cm to 1.2 m. Borders run along both long edges, from a fine 1 cm zari line to 15 cm or more on heavy silks. Standard sarees are 5.5 m without a blouse piece or about 6.3 m with one, and 44 to 47 inches wide; the nine-yard (nauvari) drape needs about 8.2 m.

Choose a starting style, then set the length, width and sections. Pick a border style (plain, temple triangles, zigzag, zari lines or a two-colour ganga-jamuna border), a body pattern (butta, stripes, checks, jaal lattice or zari dots) with its repeat and motif size, and a pallu design. The layout is drawn in true proportion, with a close-up of the pallu and border below it.

Colours and the spec sheet

Each colour field has quick swatches from the Forecut India Colour Forecast 2026–28, plus gold and silver zari and neutrals, or you can pick any colour. The spec sheet lists every section in centimetres and inches, every colour with its hex value and nearest Pantone TCX reference, and a written description of the border, body and pallu, ready to send to a weaver, printer or sampling unit. Download the layout as PNG or SVG, or print the spec sheet.

Design tips

  • Keep motif size in proportion to the repeat: a motif about a third of the repeat reads as scattered butta; larger motifs feel dense and heavy.
  • Wide contrast borders and a heavy pallu suit festive and wedding sarees; for daily and office wear, a narrow border and a light body sit closer to current trends. See the saree core item update.
  • Repeat the border colour in the blouse, or pick the accent colour for a modern contrast.

This is a planning layout, not a loom file: jacquard or dobby designs, print repeats and colour separations still need to be prepared by your weaver or print studio.
