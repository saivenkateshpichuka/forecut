Handloom quiet luxury: Chanderi, Maheshwari, khadi and tussar

Why calm, handwoven Indian fabrics are leading in 2026–27, which weaves to watch, how designers are using them, and how to build a handloom capsule at value, mid and premium prices.

By Forecut Editorial · 11 October 2026 · 6 min read

One of the clearest messages from the Indian runway in 2026 was that craft leads and ornament steps back. Designers showed handloom cotton, Chanderi, handspun khadi and tussar in loose, relaxed shapes, with minimal surface work and, in some collections, deliberately raw, unfinished edges.

Forecut calls this direction handloom quiet luxury: pieces that look special because of the cloth itself, not because of heavy embellishment.

Why it is happening

Shoppers want pieces that feel special but can be worn again and again. Handloom also carries a clear sustainability and provenance story, and the government-backed push for khadi keeps it visible. Coverage of Lakmé Fashion Week 2026 highlighted AK|OK by Anamika Khanna (handloom cotton and Chanderi, loose drapes, raw edges), Péro by Aneeth Arora (handwoven everyday wear) and Kartik Research (handspun khadi with Rabari embroidery).

The weaves to know

  • Chanderi: a light, sheer weave from Madhya Pradesh, used for kurtas, sarees and jackets. Steady and at its peak.
  • Maheshwari: fine cotton-silk handloom known for its reversible borders. Rising into the festive season.
  • Jamdani: hand-woven motifs on fine cotton. Rising.
  • Handspun khadi: now shown in silk, wool, linen and cotton, as at the Navdhara Khadi showcase. Emerging.
  • Tussar silk: a textured, natural-gold wild silk. Rising.

How it looks on product

The silhouettes are relaxed: straight kurtas with band collars, easy kurta sets, loose drapes. Embellishment moves to tone-on-tone embroidery at the neckline and cuffs rather than all-over work. Colours stay calm: ivory, sage, soft pastels and natural undyed tones.

Sourcing reality

True handloom has longer lead times and minimum orders. A practical approach is to use genuine handloom for hero pieces and tell their story clearly, while using powerloom Chanderi-look fabric or cotton-silk for value ranges.

What to do this season

  • Build a calm-colour handloom capsule: kurta sets, sarees and men's kurtas.
  • Swap heavy embroidery for tone-on-tone work at the neckline and cuffs.
  • Tell the weave and the cluster story on the label and the product page.
