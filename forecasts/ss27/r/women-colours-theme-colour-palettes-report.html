How to read the palettes

Each theme has a lead colour (the largest area of the look), an accent (used in small doses) and one or two colours from the SS27 womenswear palette that connect the theme to the rest of the range. Pantone TCX codes are the nearest published match to each colour; confirm against a physical Pantone book before dyeing. The themes themselves are described on their own pages under SS27 Women's Themes.

Why these palettes

The two new SS27 women's themes follow the runway colour evidence directly: white rose from 23.7% to 28.4% of SS27 looks and black stayed the most worn colour at 35.2%, which gives One-Layer Summer its white base and Lingerie as Outerwear its black base. Purple is the season's new colour, from Chanel's lilac to Miu Miu's royal purple, so violet becomes the lingerie theme's accent. Butter is one of the soft, calming shades named on the runways.

The six earlier SS27 themes keep the colours they were published with. Their nearest Pantone codes are listed so that they can be lab-dipped alongside the new palette.

Design

Design implications

  • Use the lead colour on at least half of the look; keep the accent to one piece or one detail.
  • Theme palettes share colours on purpose (optic white, sand, butter), so pieces from different themes can be sold together.
  • Approve one physical standard per colour before sampling, especially whites and lilac, which shift between fibres.
Buy

Commercial implications

  • Put depth into the lead colours of the two new themes (optic white, black) and keep accent colours as smaller buys.
  • Plan floor and online stories by theme, so the customer sees a complete palette in one place.
  • Carry-over colours from the earlier SS27 themes (sand, off-white) reduce dye-lot risk across deliveries.
