The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Preppy sport

School and playing-field references: rugby shirts, cricket cables, varsity jackets and oversized statement collars and cuffs.

Why now

Heritage sportswear feels familiar and gift-friendly, and collars and cuffs update basics at low cost.

Seen at

  • Polo Ralph Lauren: Sporty prep and timeless knitwear
  • Willy Chavarria: Sports prep on the runway
  • Statement collars and cuffs: Named a top menswear runway trend

Key pieces

Rugby stripes, Cable knits, Big collars; rugby stripes, short plackets.

Source: Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26; Who What Wear, AW26 trends

Roaring Twenties

1920s flapper glamour returns. Drop waists, swinging fringe, beading and long pearls lead evening, and the same codes cross into daywear through T-bar heels, velvet and pleated skirts.

Why now

Party-season demand meets nostalgia for glamour. Drop waists are easier to wear than bodycon, which helps them sell beyond evening.

Seen at

  • Chanel: Fringed silks and beaded sequins
  • Fendi: Drop-waist dresses and 1920s evening codes
  • Rabanne: Chainmail and sequin minis

Key pieces

Drop waist, Fringe, T-bar heels; drop waist with pleated skirt.

Source: Who What Wear, Autumn/Winter 2026 trends

Fully loaded

Embellishment moves from evening onto everyday pieces: sequinned jeans, beaded knits, feathers and sheer layers over heavier fabrics.

Why now

Social media rewards texture and shine, and embellishment makes basics feel special.

Key pieces

Sequins, Beadwork, Sheer layers; sequin and chainmail minis.

Source: Who What Wear, AW26 trends (the source describes the trend without naming brands)

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Boxy short-sleeve shirts

Wide, boxy short-sleeve shirts. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Grade extra ease carefully so smaller sizes do not swamp.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.
  • Fit on at least two sizes before committing to bulk.

Fabric and trims

Oxford cotton, poplin, linen blends, piqué or fine-gauge knit, with mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Check shackets

Heavy check shirt-jackets. This is a garment-level idea that can anchor a capsule.

Design notes

  • Match checks at centre front, pockets and side seams; plan for the extra fabric.
  • Start with a small capsule and track sell-through.
  • Build two price tiers of the idea.

Fabric and trims

Yarn-dyed wool or brushed cotton checks; matching adds about 10–15% fabric.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Contrast cuffs

White or contrast cuffs. A cuff detail is visible in every pose and costs little to add.

Design notes

  • Use contrast to highlight the shape; check colour fastness between shades.
  • Interface cuffs and check opening size for hand pass-through.
  • Use the cuff to repeat a trim from the front.

Fabric and trims

Oxford cotton, poplin, linen blends, piqué or fine-gauge knit, with mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Embroidered pocket shirts

Small embroidery on chest pockets. Embellishment lifts price perception and suits occasion and gifting drops.

Design notes

  • Place embroidery on flat areas; use backing to avoid puckering.
  • Test embellishment for snagging and washing.
  • Keep embellishment away from friction areas like underarms.

Fabric and trims

Oxford cotton, poplin, linen blends, piqué or fine-gauge knit, with mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Grandad collar linen shirts

Band collars on washed linen. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Approve wash and finish on bulk fabric; set clear shade bands.
  • Pre-wash linen to control shrinkage and soften the handle.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Linen or linen-cotton blends.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Heritage rugby polos

Long-sleeve rugby polos with stripes. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Match stripes at seams and centre front.
  • Keep the collar flat with a firm rib or interfacing.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Piqué, fine-gauge knit or jersey.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Boxy short-sleeve shirts
  • Check shackets
  • Contrast cuffs
  • Embroidered pocket shirts
  • Grandad collar linen shirts
  • Heritage rugby polos

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Chambray work shirts
  • Contrast-collar polos
  • Geometric panel piecing
  • Short-sleeve Oxford shirts
  • Snap-front western shirts
  • Velvet shirts

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Overshirt chest flap pockets
  • Oxford button-downs
  • Popover shirts
  • Textured knit polos

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Cuban camp collars

Actions

  • Add white contrast collars to existing shirt blocks.
  • Build one striped rugby shirt in three colourways.
  • Use tipping on knit V-necks and cuffs.
  • Lead evening with one drop-waist dress in two lengths.
  • Add fringe to hems and sleeves rather than the whole garment.
  • Pair with T-bar heels and long pearls in visual merchandising.
