SS27 Women's Couture Craft and Techniques

The July 2026 haute couture shows set the craft agenda for SS27: inflated volume and shredded gazar at Balenciaga, poured and moulded materials at Dior and Schiaparelli, and fairy-tale embroidery at Chanel.

Season
Spring Summer 2027
Audience
Women
Published
2026-11-25
When it lands

Couture ideas typically reach premium ready-to-wear within one to three seasons (Forecut assessment). Volume and waist ideas are already in SS27; seam-following embroidery is a candidate for AW27 and SS28.

The evidence

The autumn/winter 2026-27 haute couture season, shown in Paris from 6 to 9 July 2026, included 30 houses, five of them new to the calendar. The Associated Press called couture "the industry's laboratory and its loudest advertisement".

  • Balenciaga (Pierpaolo Piccioli's debut): 3D body scans used to build new mannequins; leather and cashmere moulded by hand; balloon-hemmed gazar and a strapless gown made of 24,150 shreds of gazar; hooded feather cocoons; dresses made entirely of goose and ostrich feathers, drop waists, veils and high collars.
  • Dior (Jonathan Anderson): inspired by the sculptor Lynda Benglis, whose techniques are pouring, knotting and moulding; crushed pleated hats and sheer tasselled fans.
  • Chanel (Matthieu Blazy): suits, braids, tweeds and chains, with an embroidered vine running up heels and through seams.
  • Schiaparelli (Daniel Roseberry): corsets moulded into lifelike torsos and silicone shaped by hand.
  • Iris van Herpen: some 30,000 hand-blown glass beads on sheer tulle.
  • Elie Saab: velvet corseted gowns with New Look waists.

What it means for SS27 womenswear

Three couture ideas reach ready-to-wear in SS27:

  1. Volume from light cloth. Balenciaga's gazar and feathers connect to the bubble and layered skirts of SS27 ready-to-wear, where Dior used layered organza.
  2. The defined waist. Elie Saab's New Look waists and Balenciaga's drop waists sit alongside the cinched and peplum jackets of the SS27 shows.
  3. Embroidery that follows the garment. Chanel's vine embroidery running through seams is a technique that can be scaled down to a single embroidered line on a ready-to-wear jacket or skirt.
Design

Design implications

  • Borrow one couture technique per piece and keep the rest simple.
  • Use light cloth (organza, gazar-like polyester faille) for volume rather than petticoats.
  • Place embroidery along seams and edges to reduce stitch counts.
Buy

Commercial implications

  • Couture-inspired pieces belong at the top of the range; one or two hero pieces per delivery.
  • Use couture references in storytelling and campaigns, not in pricing claims.
