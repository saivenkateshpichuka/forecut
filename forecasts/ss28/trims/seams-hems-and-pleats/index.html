The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Performance ease

Designs that feel intuitive and move easily: articulated sleeves and knees, stretch and breathable fabrics for sport and commuting.

Why now

Early SS28 previews point to performance design that removes restriction.

Key pieces

Articulation, Stretch, Breathable; articulated sleeves.

Source: FashionUnited, "SS28 Preview: The modern flow of performance design". Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Commuter tailoring

Suits built for city days: stretch suiting, soft shoulders, concealed buttons and easy-care fabrics.

Why now

Hybrid working needs tailoring that is comfortable on a commute and smart at a desk.

Key pieces

Stretch suits, Concealed buttons, Soft shoulders; stretch commuter suiting.

Source: Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Structured clarity

Consumers move away from constant novelty toward fewer, better pieces: clean column shapes, clear lines and minimal trims.

Why now

Early SS28 trade intelligence describes a demand for "better" fashion rather than more fashion.

Key pieces

Column shapes, Clean fronts, Minimal trims; clean column dresses.

Source: FashionUnited, "Spring Summer 2028 Trend Intelligence: The Rise of Structured Clarity". Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Ankle-strap balloon trousers

Loose balloon-leg trousers gathered at the ankle by a cinchable strap; Ralph Lauren Pre-Fall 2027, 6 Nov 2026. The hem finish changes the mood of a piece with minimal pattern work.

Design notes

  • Shape the volume through curved side seams and darts; check on several body types.
  • Cut on the bias for drape and pin-test gathers on the stand.
  • Test hem finish after washing for twisting or fraying.

Fabric and trims

Fluid crepe, jersey or satin.

Where to use it

Best on wide-leg trousers, tailored pants, midi and mini skirts. Works in womenswear trousers and skirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Dresses, rising

Bubble-hem dresses

Short dresses with a puffed, gathered hem. The hem finish changes the mood of a piece with minimal pattern work.

Design notes

  • Shape the volume through curved side seams and darts; check on several body types.
  • Cut on the bias for drape and pin-test gathers on the stand.
  • Test hem finish after washing for twisting or fraying.

Fabric and trims

Fluid crepe, jersey or satin.

Where to use it

Best on midi day dresses, shirt dresses, slip dresses and occasion styles. Works in womenswear dresses as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Dresses, rising

Corset-seamed dresses

Visible corset-style seams on day dresses. Seam placement adds structure and visual lines without extra trims.

Design notes

  • Use topstitching to make seam lines visible.
  • Check seam strength at stress points.

Fabric and trims

Viscose crepe, cotton poplin, satin-back crepe or jersey, with self-covered buttons and invisible zips.

Where to use it

Best on midi day dresses, shirt dresses, slip dresses and occasion styles. Works in womenswear dresses as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Denim, rising

Cuffed jean hems

Turned-up hems on straight jeans. The hem finish changes the mood of a piece with minimal pattern work.

Design notes

  • Approve wash and finish on bulk fabric; set clear shade bands.
  • Test hem finish after washing for twisting or fraying.
  • Keep hem treatment consistent within a capsule.

Fabric and trims

11–14 oz cotton or cotton-lyocell denim, with copper or antique-brass rivets and shank buttons.

Where to use it

Best on jeans, denim jackets, skirts and shirts. Works in womenswear denim as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Cuffed wide trousers

Wide legs finished with deep turn-ups. The hem finish changes the mood of a piece with minimal pattern work.

Design notes

  • Use a fabric with enough weight and drape so the leg falls cleanly.
  • Test hem finish after washing for twisting or fraying.
  • Keep hem treatment consistent within a capsule.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend suiting, cotton twill, crepe or satin, with hook-and-bar closures and concealed zips.

Where to use it

Best on wide-leg trousers, tailored pants, midi and mini skirts. Works in womenswear trousers and skirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Dresses, rising

Pleated midi dresses

Knife-pleated skirts on fitted bodices. Pleats add volume and movement while keeping a tailored finish.

Design notes

  • Use pressed pleats for tailoring and soft pleats for relaxed styles.
  • Press and tack pleats so they hold through wear.
  • Balance pleat depth with hip ease.

Fabric and trims

Viscose crepe, cotton poplin, satin-back crepe or jersey, with self-covered buttons and invisible zips.

Where to use it

Best on midi day dresses, shirt dresses, slip dresses and occasion styles. Works in womenswear dresses as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Ankle-strap balloon trousers
  • Bubble-hem dresses
  • Corset-seamed dresses
  • Cuffed jean hems
  • Cuffed wide trousers
  • Pleated midi dresses

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Articulated-knee trousers
  • Bonded, raw-cut seams

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Fringed minis with back trains
  • Scale-beaded mermaid hems
  • Bubble hems
  • Deep turn-ups
  • Double front pleats, sharp creases
  • Puffed pleated suit trousers

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Knit fringe at hems
  • Lace-trimmed hems
  • Pressed front seam
  • Shorts with turn-ups
  • Single and double pleats
  • Unfinished raw hems

Actions

  • Add stretch to core tailoring fabrics.
  • Introduce articulated elbows on outerwear.
  • Test breathability claims before marketing them.
  • Launch a machine-washable suit.
  • Use soft, unlined construction.
  • Market comfort and care, not just looks.
