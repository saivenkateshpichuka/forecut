SS27 Women's One-Layer Summer

A summer dressed for heat: sheer suits, featherweight organza tailoring, single-layer knits and perforated leather. Lightness replaces layering as the sign of luxury.

When it lands

In stores February to July 2027, strongest for the high-summer drop. Forecut expects single-layer tailoring to remain relevant as summers get hotter.

The story

Europe's extreme summer heat shaped the SS27 collections. Nicolas Ghesquière put it simply at Louis Vuitton: "We needed to think differently. You want one layer". His collection cut tailoring from the lightest knits and used fabrics as thin as cigarette paper. At Dior, Jonathan Anderson made featherweight organza tailoring printed to look like wool suiting, with pleated plissé and petals, and sheer suits with mesh blazers. Hermès perforated suede for airflow and showed sheer knits. In Milan, Armani, Missoni, Max Mara and Ferragamo showed see-through dresses and veiled skirts and trousers.

The result is a new idea of summer polish: a complete, tailored look that weighs almost nothing.

Colours

Optic white, butter and lilac from the SS27 womenswear palette, with pale tones that show the transparency of the cloth. See SS27 Women's Season Colour Analysis for codes.

Silhouettes

  • Sheer suits: unlined jackets and straight or full skirts in organza or mesh.
  • Single-layer knit tailoring: a fine knit jacket worn alone.
  • Layered midi skirts in two or three sheer layers.
  • Sheer fine-gauge tops over slips or bandeaus.

Fabrics

Organza, chiffon, tulle and mesh; nylon organza, double gauze and devoré chiffon; fine wool and extra-fine merino; perforated leather and suede.

Trims and details

Narrow rolled hems, French seams, ribbon binding, organza petals, minimal hardware.

Images

Make

Products and customers

  • Sheer organza jacket and skirt sets; mesh blazers; fine knit jackets.
  • Sheer layered midi skirts; sheer blouses with matching slips.
  • Customers: city customers in hot climates who still dress for work and occasions; resort and holiday buyers.
Design

Design implications

  • Design every sheer piece with its under-layer.
  • Keep construction invisible: French seams, rolled hems, no heavy facings.
  • Print or weave tailoring codes (pinstripes, checks) onto light cloth to keep a suit reading as a suit.
Buy

Commercial implications

  • Sell sheer suits as sets with under-layers to raise the average order.
  • Market to hot-climate regions (Gulf, India, Southeast Asia, Australia) with modest styling options.
  • Watch return rates; show what is worn underneath on product pages.
