Spring/Summer 2027, Women, Silhouettes, Woven

SS27 Women's Woven Silhouette Direction

The skirt replaces the loose trouser as the anchor of the spring wardrobe. Straight and pencil skirts at knee to mid-calf, the dropped-waist dress and the short, waisted jacket lead; bubble volume is a styling moment rather than a volume buy.

The evidence

The clearest signal of the season is measurable. Across 12,320 SS27 womenswear looks, skirts rose from 19.3% to 27.9% of looks against SS26, while dresses fell from 37.0% to 33.3%[1]. The fastest-rising key item was the straight skirt, which doubled from 5.1% to 10.3%; the dropped-waist dress grew from 0.6% to 4.9%[1]. On the other side of the ledger, loose trousers fell from 11.5% to 3.0% of looks, chinos from 6.7% to 1.5% and mini dresses from 9.8% to 4.8%[1].

The shows explain the numbers. Prada put a skirt in every look, treating it, in the house's words, as "this unique and universal garment"[2][3]. Chanel cut narrow skirts to just below the knee, the length Gabrielle Chanel considered most flattering[2], in smocked tweed and sheer organza[3]. At Dior, Jonathan Anderson ran the full range, from gauze-light minis to ankle-length, flared, pleated and tulle-trimmed skirts[4]. Across Milan and Paris the dominant lengths were knee and mid-calf[4].

Retail buyers read it the same way. Elodie Abrial, commercial director of the Le Bon Marché group, described skirts with "a great deal of fluidity and movement"[2]. Victoria Dartigues, director of womenswear buying at Galeries Lafayette Paris, linked the season's move to separates to wardrobes that "encourage self-creation rather than adopting a uniform"[2].

Why it matters

The shift is structural rather than decorative. Designers rebuilt the season around separates: Daniel Roseberry at Schiaparelli said the house reinvented its dresses and necklines "as tops and bottoms"[2]. A wardrobe built from separates sells in more combinations, which is why department stores are framing it as a buying opportunity rather than a runway story.

It also follows two seasons of very relaxed trousers. With loose trousers and chinos falling sharply in runway share[1], the skirt takes the role of the easy, comfortable bottom, and tailored trousers hold steady at 12.4% of looks[1]. Heat is the third driver: designers were explicit that the collections answered Europe's summer temperatures with lighter, single-layer clothes[3].

Shapes to lead with

Straight and pencil skirt, knee to mid-calf. The volume driver of the season. Straight and pencil shapes led in Milan[5]; Saint Laurent paired gold pencil skirts with trench coats[5]. Build it as a core shape in suiting, satin and denim.

The full, layered midi skirt. Dior, Chanel and the Australian label Aje showed long, light, layered skirts[5]; Prada used layered crinolines, organza shells and tulle[4]. Commercially, this works best in two or three layers of light cloth rather than structured petticoats.

The dropped-waist dress. Back on the runway[5] and among the fastest risers[1]. Pair the dropped seam with a pleated or flared skirt; keep the bodice relaxed.

The short, waisted jacket. Cropped jackets replaced oversized shapes at Jil Sander, Tod's and Marni, while Armani, Gucci and Jil Sander cinched jackets at the waist[4]. Oversized blazers were scarce in Milan[5]. Givenchy showed cinched peplum tailoring[3].

Bias-cut slips and 1930s gowns. Chanel's collection included bias-cut 1930s gowns[4]; satin slips carry the same line into day wear.

Shapes to handle with care

The pouf and bubble skirt. Christian Lacroix's mid-1980s shape returned at Loewe and Dior, in very short versions at Miu Miu and Zimmermann, and as a denim sculpture at Comme des Garçons[2]. FashionUnited's report notes the trend may fade quickly because the pieces are more image-driven than wearable and are almost impossible to sit in[2]. Forecut's assessment: use bubble hems for editorial and occasion pieces, not for core volume.

Micro hems and the mini dress. Etro, Roberto Cavalli and Gucci showed micro hems[4], but the mini dress halved its share of looks[1]. Keep minis as a small, younger-customer option.

Very loose trousers and chinos. The sharpest fallers of the season[1]. Reduce depth and move the customer to a straight, tailored trouser; a relaxed straight leg is replacing the curved barrel jean in denim[5].

Timing

In stores February to July 2027. The straight skirt and the waisted jacket are rising now; Forecut expects both to carry into AW27 in heavier cloth. The bubble skirt is likely to peak within SS27 itself and should not be carried forward in depth. The dropped-waist dress is at an early stage: test in SS27, build in SS28 if sell-through supports it.

Products and customers

  • Skirts: straight and pencil skirts at knee and mid-calf; pleated and layered midi skirts; satin slip skirts; denim midi skirts.
  • Dresses: dropped-waist dresses with pleated skirts; bias-cut slip dresses; long column dresses for evening.
  • Jackets: cropped, waisted jackets; one-button cinched jackets; peplum jackets.
  • Customers: the core is the working woman who buys separates and builds a wardrobe over seasons, the customer Galeries Lafayette describes. Younger customers will pick up the bubble hem and the mini in small doses.

Design implications

  • Set the lengths first. Plan skirts at three lengths: just below the knee, mid-calf and ankle. The knee and mid-calf lengths carry the volume[4].
  • Engineer movement. Narrow knee-length skirts need a walking vent, kick pleat or slit; check stride and sitting in the first fitting.
  • Waist placement matters again. Jackets stop at the natural waist or are belted there; dropped-waist dresses move the seam to the hip. Avoid long, unshaped blazers.
  • Finish carries the price. Waistbands, linings and hems decide quality in a skirt; fit depends on waist and hip grading, and bias-cut styles should hang before hemming[5].
  • Build in pairs. Design skirts and jackets to work together and apart, reflecting the season's separates logic[2].

Commercial implications

  • Move budget from trousers to skirts. With loose trousers and chinos falling sharply[1], shift part of the trouser buy into straight and midi skirts while holding a tailored trouser as the core.
  • Expect better multi-buys. Separates encourage customers to buy two or three pieces that work together; plan coordinated skirt and jacket stories in shared colours.
  • Cap the bubble skirt. Treat it as a short-run fashion piece with a clear exit date[2].
  • Reduce mini dresses in favour of midi and maxi lengths; maxi dresses held a 16.0% share of looks[1].
  • Price ladder: core straight skirts at entry and mid price in suiting and denim; layered organza and satin midi skirts at a premium.
