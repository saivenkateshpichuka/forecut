Design notes

How to use the trims.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, emerging

Fly-front placket shirts

Hidden-button plackets for a clean front. A placket update freshens the front without touching the pattern block.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Interface plackets so they lie flat after laundering.
  • Match buttonhole spacing to the bust or chest line to avoid gaping.

Fabric and trims

Oxford cotton, poplin, linen blends, piqué or fine-gauge knit, with mother-of-pearl effect buttons. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Knitwear, emerging

Mock-neck fine knits

Fine knits with a low mock neck. Changing the neckline is one of the cheapest ways to update a proven block.

Design notes

  • Keep the funnel high but soft at the front so it is comfortable under the chin.
  • Check neck opening against head circumference on pull-on styles.
  • Stay-stitch or tape the neckline so it does not stretch out.

Fabric and trims

Merino, lambswool, cotton or recycled wool blends in 7–12 gauge, with self-yarn or horn buttons.

Where to use it

Best on crew necks, quarter-zips, cardigans and knit polos. Works in menswear knitwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Outerwear, emerging

Taped-seam commuter jackets

City jackets with bonded, taped seams. Seam placement adds structure and visual lines without extra trims.

Design notes

  • Use topstitching to make seam lines visible.
  • Check seam strength at stress points.

Fabric and trims

Wool melton, waxed or brushed cotton, recycled nylon ripstop, with matte metal zips and snaps.

Where to use it

Best on overcoats, bombers, chore jackets, puffers and overshirts. Works in menswear outerwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, fading

Contrast cuffs

White or contrast cuffs. A cuff detail is visible in every pose and costs little to add.

Design notes

  • Use contrast to highlight the shape; check colour fastness between shades.
  • Interface cuffs and check opening size for hand pass-through.
  • Use the cuff to repeat a trim from the front.

Fabric and trims

Oxford cotton, poplin, linen blends, piqué or fine-gauge knit, with mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.
