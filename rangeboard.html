How to build a balanced collection

A collection plan is a list of style slots before it is a list of designs. Decide how many styles each category needs (for example dresses, tops, trousers and outerwear), give every slot a target price, the colourways and size set, the units per SKU and a drop date, and the planner totals units and production cost for the whole range. When planned production cost goes over your budget, the planner says by how much, so you can cut a colourway or reduce depth before samples are ordered rather than after.

Each style can carry a trend stage from a Forecut forecast. A healthy range usually keeps most depth in rising and peaking details that customers already recognise, a small share in emerging details to test, and very little in anything marked fading. The planner groups styles by category and by trend stage, so it is easy to see if one category holds too much of the money or if the whole range leans on one idea.

Reading the plan by market

  • India: plan festive and wedding drops around Dussehra, Diwali and the winter wedding months, with deeper size runs in kurta sets and co-ords than in occasion lehengas.
  • Europe and North America: split autumn into two or three drops so outerwear and knitwear arrive as the weather turns, with a party drop before Black Friday and Christmas.
  • Gulf and Southeast Asia: Ramadan and Eid capsules need modest silhouettes and earlier delivery than western spring ranges.
  • Southern hemisphere: summer ranges land from September, so the same trend reaches stores about six months ahead of the north.
