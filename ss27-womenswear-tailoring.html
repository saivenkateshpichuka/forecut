The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

The waist returns

After years of oversized shapes the waist is back. Peplums, basque waists, balloon and bubble shapes and belted or wrapped fronts define the body again.

Why now

Forecasters see a return to elegance and shape. A defined waist refreshes existing silhouettes without new categories.

Seen at

  • Li Edelkoort, "Craft of Black": Wraps and belts the waist tightly
  • SS27 bridal and evening: Corseted bodices, basque waists and bubble skirts
  • Double belt: Returns as a waist accessory

Key pieces

Peplum, Basque waist, Bubble hem; basque waist bodices.

Source: FashionUnited, Spring/Summer 2027 trend report

Elegant ease

Dressing gets refined again. The tie returns, shirts are tucked in, trousers are pleated and slightly puffed, and elegant mules and pointed flats replace sneakers.

Why now

Fabric forecasters say street style and sneakers are giving way to elegance.

Seen at

  • Loewe: The return of the tie
  • Munich Fabric Start forecasters: "Everything is becoming elegant"
  • SS27 menswear: Slimmer ties, pleated and puffed trousers

Key pieces

Slim ties, Mules, Pointed flats; tucked shirts with slim ties.

Source: FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage and SS27 trend report

In motion

Long fringe that moves with every step, and bold head-to-toe stripes, from rainbow to tonal.

Why now

Movement and stripes photograph well for video-led social media.

Seen at

  • Luar, Kim Shui, Ulla Johnson, Heirlome, Monse: Long, figure-covering fringe
  • DVF: Rainbow stripes with long shorts
  • Proenza Schouler, Tommy Hilfiger: Head-to-toe stripes

Key pieces

Fringe, Stripes, Swing; fringed hems.

Source: The Zoe Report, NYFW SS27 trends

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Black tailoring with fuchsia accents

Black wool and corduroy suiting with fuchsia kept as the only accent colour, never mixed in one outfit; Reserved x Dawid Woliński capsule, Europe, 5 Nov 2026. A colour-led detail that is easy to roll across many styles.

Design notes

  • Choose wale size for the garment: fine wale for shirts, wider wale for trousers and jackets.
  • Keep the jacket and trousers in the same cloth so they sell as a set.
  • Lab-dip early and approve under daylight and store lighting.

Fabric and trims

Cotton corduroy in 8–14 wale.

Where to use it

Best on blazers, waistcoats, suit trousers and co-ord sets. Works in womenswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Tone-on-tone embroidered tuxedos

Black smoking suits embroidered all over in black, worn as the alternative to a gown; Marian Saud for Débora Plager at the Martín Fierro de Cable awards, Buenos Aires, 12 Nov 2026. Embellishment lifts price perception and suits occasion and gifting drops.

Design notes

  • Use laser finishing for consistent, lower-impact distressing.
  • Place embroidery on flat areas; use backing to avoid puckering.
  • Test embellishment for snagging and washing.

Fabric and trims

Merino or lambswool in 5–7 gauge.

Where to use it

Best on blazers, waistcoats, suit trousers and co-ord sets. Works in womenswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Bermuda suit shorts

Tailored shorts worn with blazers. Length shifts are simple to apply across existing blocks.

Design notes

  • Use laser finishing for consistent, lower-impact distressing.
  • Keep the jacket and trousers in the same cloth so they sell as a set.
  • Update length on existing blocks rather than new patterns.

Fabric and trims

Tropical wool, wool-viscose suiting or linen blends, with half-canvas fronts and horn buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blazers, waistcoats, suit trousers and co-ord sets. Works in womenswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Check tailoring

Prince of Wales and windowpane checks. The fabric story carries this detail, so sampling and handle are key.

Design notes

  • Match checks at centre front, pockets and side seams; plan for the extra fabric.
  • Request hand-feel swatches and test for pilling, shrinkage and colour fastness.
  • Lock fabric minimums early; specialist fabrics have longer lead times.

Fabric and trims

Yarn-dyed wool or brushed cotton checks; matching adds about 10–15% fabric.

Where to use it

Best on blazers, waistcoats, suit trousers and co-ord sets. Works in womenswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Cinched one-button jackets

Nipped waists with a single button. Waist treatment controls the silhouette and how the garment flatters.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Mark waist position on the block and check on several body shapes.
  • Offer adjustable or elastic options for size inclusivity.

Fabric and trims

Tropical wool, wool-viscose suiting or linen blends, with half-canvas fronts and horn buttons. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blazers, waistcoats, suit trousers and co-ord sets. Works in womenswear tailoring as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tailoring, rising

Collarless double-breasted jackets

Double-breasted fronts without lapels. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Balance lapel width with shoulder width and garment length.
  • Space buttons evenly and balance the wrap width.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Black tailoring with fuchsia accents
  • Tone-on-tone embroidered tuxedos
  • Bermuda suit shorts
  • Check tailoring
  • Cinched one-button jackets
  • Collarless double-breasted jackets

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Armour-structured vests
  • Collarless tailored jackets
  • Wide-leg suit trousers

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Belted trench blazers
  • Cropped blazers
  • Sharp pointed shoulders

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Nothing at this stage

Actions

  • Add a peplum version of your best-selling blouse.
  • Cut a basque-waist seam into a core dress block.
  • Sell double belts as an add-on accessory.
  • Add slim ties and pocket squares as add-on sales.
  • Style shirts tucked into pleated trousers in imagery.
  • Shift footwear mix from sneakers toward mules and flats.
