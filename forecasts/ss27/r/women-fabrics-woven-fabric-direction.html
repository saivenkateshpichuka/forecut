SS27 / Fabrics / Women

SS27 Women's Woven Fabric Direction

Two families carry the season: crisp, dense silks and satins with real body, and very light sheers that let a whole look stay as one layer. Fine wools, linens and refined cottons hold the tailoring and shirting core.

The evidence

Satin appeared in 20.8% of SS27 womenswear looks, up 3.0 points, second only to twill at 26.2%[1]. Buyers in Paris singled out duchesse satin and high-shine finishes[2]. Première Vision had already set out the two silk directions in February: "Couture Density" (radzimirs, mikados, duchesse satins, faille, piqué damasks and weighted silks, crisp and substantial) and "Opalescent Lightness" (crepes, devoré chiffons, nylon organzas and double gauzes)[3].

On the runway, lightness was the stronger story. Dior showed featherweight organza tailoring printed to look like classic wool suiting[4] and sheer suits with mesh blazers[5]. Louis Vuitton used fabrics as thin as cigarette paper[5]; Hermès perforated suede for airflow[4]. In Milan, Giorgio Armani, Missoni, Max Mara and Ferragamo showed see-through dresses and veiled skirts and trousers in chiffon, tulle, mesh and organza[5].

A third, smaller story is gloss: vinyl and translucent PVC, latex-effect rubber, shiny nylon and organza at Ferragamo, Prada, MSGM, Jil Sander and others, and latex coated in gold leaf at Saint Laurent[5].

Why it matters

The silhouette story depends on cloth. A straight skirt at the knee needs a fabric with body; a single-layer summer look needs a fabric that is light but not flimsy. That is why the two extremes, dense silk and featherweight sheer, rise together. Première Vision also notes satins, double satins and taffetas moving towards stretch[6], which makes structured skirts and fitted jackets easier to wear.

Timing

Dense satins and sheers are in SS27 production now. Forecut expects dense silks to carry into AW27 occasion wear, while the very light sheers are a summer story. Coated and vinyl fabrics are early: use as accents.

Products and customers

  • Satin: slip skirts, bias-cut dresses, wide trousers, jabot and bow blouses.
  • Organza and chiffon: sheer suits and overlays, layered midi skirts, sheer sleeves.
  • Fine wool and double crepe: cropped, waisted jackets and straight skirts for the working wardrobe.
  • Linen: jackets, skirts and trousers for high summer and resort markets.

Design implications

  • Pair weights deliberately. Combine one structured fabric (duchesse, faille, crepe) with one sheer (organza, chiffon) in the same colour.
  • Sheers need clean construction. Use French seams and narrow rolled hems; satin needs fine needles and careful pressing[2].
  • Print the illusion. Dior's organza printed to look like wool[4] shows a way to keep tailoring codes in a summer weight.
  • Check fibre content. Much commercial satin is polyester[2]; state composition clearly and test pilling and seam slippage on satin and organza.

Commercial implications

  • Price on fibre and finish. Silk duchesse and mikado justify a premium; polyester satins should sit at entry price with clear labelling.
  • Plan matched colour. Head-to-toe colour and satin-with-sheer pairings need every fabric and trim lab-dipped to one shade before bulk.
  • Order sheers with linings. Budget for slips and under-layers so sheer pieces sell as complete looks.
  • Keep vinyl and PVC small. Accent pieces only, with careful care labelling.
