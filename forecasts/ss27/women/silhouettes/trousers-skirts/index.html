The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

The waist returns

After years of oversized shapes the waist is back. Peplums, basque waists, balloon and bubble shapes and belted or wrapped fronts define the body again.

Why now

Forecasters see a return to elegance and shape. A defined waist refreshes existing silhouettes without new categories.

Seen at

  • Li Edelkoort, "Craft of Black": Wraps and belts the waist tightly
  • SS27 bridal and evening: Corseted bodices, basque waists and bubble skirts
  • Double belt: Returns as a waist accessory

Key pieces

Peplum, Basque waist, Bubble hem; basque waist bodices.

Source: FashionUnited, Spring/Summer 2027 trend report

Undone

A deliberately unfinished look: straps falling off the shoulder, exposed or unhooked undergarments, top layers left unsewn and raw, frayed hems on muslin-like cottons.

Why now

A relaxed, personal take on elegance, and raw edges suit natural fabrics.

Seen at

  • Altuzarra: Deshabille looks
  • Christian Cowan: Undone layers
  • Coach and Prabal Gurung: Muslin looks
  • LII: Double-layer minis
  • Paloma Wool: Dropped linings

Key pieces

Raw hems, Slip straps, Muslin; unfinished raw hems.

Source: The Zoe Report, NYFW SS27 trends; FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage

Elegant ease

Dressing gets refined again. The tie returns, shirts are tucked in, trousers are pleated and slightly puffed, and elegant mules and pointed flats replace sneakers.

Why now

Fabric forecasters say street style and sneakers are giving way to elegance.

Seen at

  • Loewe: The return of the tie
  • Munich Fabric Start forecasters: "Everything is becoming elegant"
  • SS27 menswear: Slimmer ties, pleated and puffed trousers

Key pieces

Slim ties, Mules, Pointed flats; tucked shirts with slim ties.

Source: FashionUnited, SS27 Paris coverage and SS27 trend report

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Balloon trousers

Volume gathered into the ankle. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Shape the volume through curved side seams and darts; check on several body types.
  • Cut on the bias for drape and pin-test gathers on the stand.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.

Fabric and trims

Fluid crepe, jersey or satin.

Where to use it

Best on wide-leg trousers, tailored pants, midi and mini skirts. Works in womenswear trousers and skirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Barrel-leg trousers

Curved legs that taper at the hem. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Shape the volume through curved side seams and darts; check on several body types.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.
  • Fit on at least two sizes before committing to bulk.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend suiting, cotton twill, crepe or satin, with hook-and-bar closures and concealed zips.

Where to use it

Best on wide-leg trousers, tailored pants, midi and mini skirts. Works in womenswear trousers and skirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Bermuda shorts

Knee-length tailored shorts with turn-ups. Length shifts are simple to apply across existing blocks.

Design notes

  • Update length on existing blocks rather than new patterns.
  • Check length on petite and tall fits.

Bubble hems

Puffed hems on short skirts. The hem finish changes the mood of a piece with minimal pattern work.

Design notes

  • Shape the volume through curved side seams and darts; check on several body types.
  • Test hem finish after washing for twisting or fraying.
  • Keep hem treatment consistent within a capsule.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Button-front midi skirts

Buttons running down the front of the skirt. The closure changes how the garment opens, fits and is styled.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Use engineered mesh or knit for breathability.
  • Test closures for ease of use and stress points at fit stage.

Fabric and trims

Wool-blend suiting, cotton twill, crepe or satin, with hook-and-bar closures and concealed zips. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on wide-leg trousers, tailored pants, midi and mini skirts. Works in womenswear trousers and skirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Trousers & Skirts, rising

Cropped ankle trousers

Slim trousers cut above the ankle. Length shifts are simple to apply across existing blocks.

Design notes

  • Check the crop length against high-rise bottoms.
  • Update length on existing blocks rather than new patterns.
  • Check length on petite and tall fits.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Balloon trousers
  • Barrel-leg trousers
  • Bermuda shorts
  • Bubble hems
  • Button-front midi skirts
  • Cropped ankle trousers

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Ankle-strap balloon trousers
  • Dropped-crotch Aladdin trousers
  • Fringed minis with back trains
  • Low-rise slip skirts with bra tops
  • Double-belted waistbands
  • Long satin boxer shorts

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Cargo midi skirts
  • Flat-front straight trousers
  • Mesh midi skirts

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Low-slung belted waists

Actions

  • Add a peplum version of your best-selling blouse.
  • Cut a basque-waist seam into a core dress block.
  • Sell double belts as an add-on accessory.
  • Test raw hems in wash trials before bulk.
  • Layer a longer slip under existing dresses.
  • Style straps off-shoulder in imagery, not just in the garment.
