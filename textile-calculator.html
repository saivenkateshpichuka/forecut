How to use the textile calculator

Fabric needed starts from typical consumption for a size M garment at 44 and 58 inch widths, then adjusts for the width you choose, the size, shrinkage and cutting wastage, and multiplies by the number of pieces. If your pattern master has already laid a marker, type your own consumption per piece and the calculator applies only shrinkage, wastage and quantity. Add a price per metre to see the fabric cost per piece and for the order.

GSM and weight works out grams per square metre from a swatch, converts ounces per square yard (common for denim) to GSM, and turns a GSM, width and length into the weight of a roll or an order. It also converts between price per kilo and price per metre, which helps when a knit mill quotes by the kilo and your costing is by the metre.

Yarn count converts between Ne, Nm, tex, denier and dtex using the standard formulas: tex = 590.5 ÷ Ne, Nm = 1000 ÷ tex, denier = 9 × tex and dtex = 10 × tex. The woven estimate gives a greige fabric weight from ends and picks per inch, yarn counts and crimp.

Shrinkage turns a wash test into a percentage and tells you how much longer to cut. Costing and MRP adds fabric, trims, making, packing and overheads, then your margin, the retailer's margin and GST, to suggest a retail price.

Good to know

  • Wider fabric is usually cheaper per garment even when the price per metre is higher: compare the fabric cost per piece, not the price per metre.
  • Directional prints, one-way pile (velvet, corduroy) and stripes or checks that must match can add 10–20% to consumption.
  • Order a little extra for shade variation between rolls, and cut a whole order from the same dye lot where you can.
