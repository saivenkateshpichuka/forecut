How to use the pattern block maker

Choose a block, start from a size or enter your own measurements, then adjust the fit and style options. The drawing updates as you type. Every piece is drawn at true scale in centimetres: one grid square is one centimetre, and the small 5 cm square lets you check the scale after printing.

The kurta block drafts a front and back, each cut on the fold, from bust, shoulder width and armhole depth, with your choice of fit ease, straight or waist-shaped sides, neckline, hem flare and side slit. The sleeve is solved to fit: the tool measures the front and back armhole curves and sets the cap height so the sleeve head is about 1 cm longer than the armhole, the small amount of ease that lets it set in smoothly.

The skirt block drafts a front (on the fold) and back (with a centre back zip) from waist, hip and the waist-to-hip depth, with darts that share the waist shaping with the side seam. Add flare at the hem for an A-line.

Printing and using the pattern

  • Download SVG to send to a plotter or open in Illustrator, Inkscape or CLO. The file is sized in centimetres.
  • Print on A4 tiles splits the sheet into numbered tiles. Print at 100% or “actual size”, never “fit to page”, then check the 5 cm square and tape the tiles together in order (A1, A2 across, then B1…).
  • Always make a toile in calico or muslin first, check the fit on the body, and transfer any changes to the pattern before cutting good fabric.

Limits

These are simplified blocks for learning, quick samples and first toiles. They do not include grading, facings, plackets, pockets or collars, and every body is different. For production, a pattern maker should true the seams, add notches and grade the pattern across sizes. See also our guides on fabrics and couture construction.
