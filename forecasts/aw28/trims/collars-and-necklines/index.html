The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Hidden closures

Magnetic and concealed fastenings give clean, uninterrupted fronts on coats, shirts and blazers.

Why now

Supports the minimal, structured look and is easier to fasten.

Key pieces

Magnets, Concealed plackets, Hidden zips; hidden magnetic closures.

Source: Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Functional tailoring

Tailoring that works hard: belted jackets, useful pockets, clean seams and cargo details in wool.

Why now

Continues SS28 performance thinking into winter suiting.

Key pieces

Belted jackets, Utility pockets, Clean seams; belted suit jackets.

Source: Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Technical wool

Wool that stretches, sheds rain and resists creases, for suits and coats that travel well.

Why now

Natural fibre with performance benefits fits the "fewer, better" shift.

Key pieces

Stretch wool, Water-resistant, Travel suits; technical wool suiting.

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Menswear, Shirts & Polos, rising

Contrast-collar polos

Polos with contrast collars. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Keep the collar flat with a firm rib or interfacing.
  • Use contrast to highlight the shape; check colour fastness between shades.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Piqué, fine-gauge knit or jersey.

Where to use it

Best on Oxford shirts, camp-collar shirts, knit and piqué polos. Works in menswear shirts and polos as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Funnel-neck base layers

Fine ribbed funnel necks for layering. Changing the neckline is one of the cheapest ways to update a proven block.

Design notes

  • Keep the funnel high but soft at the front so it is comfortable under the chin.
  • Use a rib with good recovery so it holds its shape.
  • Check neck opening against head circumference on pull-on styles.

Fabric and trims

Cotton-elastane or merino rib.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Dresses, rising

Funnel-neck knit dresses

Ribbed knit dresses with a high funnel neck. Changing the neckline is one of the cheapest ways to update a proven block.

Design notes

  • Keep the funnel high but soft at the front so it is comfortable under the chin.
  • Use a rib with good recovery so it holds its shape.
  • Check neck opening against head circumference on pull-on styles.

Fabric and trims

Cotton-elastane or merino rib.

Where to use it

Best on midi day dresses, shirt dresses, slip dresses and occasion styles. Works in womenswear dresses as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Outerwear, rising

Funnel-neck puffers

Puffers with tall funnel necks. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Plan channel width with the silhouette: wider channels look bolder, narrow channels look more refined.
  • Keep the funnel high but soft at the front so it is comfortable under the chin.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Recycled nylon or matte peached shells with recycled polyester wadding.

Where to use it

Best on overcoats, bombers, chore jackets, puffers and overshirts. Works in menswear outerwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Dresses, rising

Halter-neck dresses

Necklines tied at the back of the neck. Changing the neckline is one of the cheapest ways to update a proven block.

Design notes

  • Add silicone tape or internal stays so the garment stays in place.
  • Check neck opening against head circumference on pull-on styles.
  • Stay-stitch or tape the neckline so it does not stretch out.

Fabric and trims

Viscose crepe, cotton poplin, satin-back crepe or jersey, with self-covered buttons and invisible zips.

Where to use it

Best on midi day dresses, shirt dresses, slip dresses and occasion styles. Works in womenswear dresses as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Henley plackets

Short button plackets on jersey tops. A placket update freshens the front without touching the pattern block.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Interface plackets so they lie flat after laundering.
  • Match buttonhole spacing to the bust or chest line to avoid gaping.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Contrast-collar polos
  • Funnel-neck base layers
  • Funnel-neck knit dresses
  • Funnel-neck puffers
  • Halter-neck dresses
  • Henley plackets

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Knitted hoods and snoods

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Lacquered funnel-neck trenches
  • Off-shoulder ruffle dresses
  • Band-collar shirts
  • Band-collar shirts with long placket
  • Collarless tailored jackets
  • Concealed-placket shirts

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Off-shoulder tweed sheath dresses
  • Chunky rib turtlenecks
  • Chunky shawl-collar cardigans
  • Detachable hoods
  • Fur and shearling collars
  • Heavy shawl-collar cardigans

Actions

  • Test magnets for washing and safety.
  • Use fly-front plackets on core shirts.
  • Market ease of fastening.
  • Add one useful pocket to core tailoring.
  • Belt a suit jacket.
  • Offer a utility vest as a layering piece.
