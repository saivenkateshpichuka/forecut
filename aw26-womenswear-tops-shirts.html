The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Preppy sport

School and playing-field references: rugby shirts, cricket cables, varsity jackets and oversized statement collars and cuffs.

Why now

Heritage sportswear feels familiar and gift-friendly, and collars and cuffs update basics at low cost.

Seen at

  • Polo Ralph Lauren: Sporty prep and timeless knitwear
  • Willy Chavarria: Sports prep on the runway
  • Statement collars and cuffs: Named a top menswear runway trend

Key pieces

Rugby stripes, Cable knits, Big collars; rugby stripes, short plackets.

Source: Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26; Who What Wear, AW26 trends

Power tailoring

Sharp, slick suiting in pinstripes and slate grey, with pointed shoulders, peak lapels and double-breasted fronts. Oxblood accents and polished heels finish the look.

Why now

Return-to-office dressing and a move away from very oversized blazers toward sharper fits.

Seen at

  • Gucci: Horsebit pumps with sharp suiting
  • Classic-fit suits (market): Up 44% in market adoption
  • Double-breasted suits (market): Up 4% year on year; pointed shoulders up 12%

Key pieces

Pinstripe, Peak lapels, Pointed shoulders; sharp pointed shoulders.

Source: Who What Wear (AW26 trends); Trendalytics, top menswear runway trends FW26

Fully loaded

Embellishment moves from evening onto everyday pieces: sequinned jeans, beaded knits, feathers and sheer layers over heavier fabrics.

Why now

Social media rewards texture and shine, and embellishment makes basics feel special.

Key pieces

Sequins, Beadwork, Sheer layers; sequin and chainmail minis.

Source: Who What Wear, AW26 trends (the source describes the trend without naming brands)

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Velvet cape tops

Evening tops with a short cape layer in place of sleeves, in burgundy velvet; Mint Velvet holiday 2026, with Sézane x Lucia Zolea's Angeline cape in the same round-up (18 Nov 2026). Sleeve volume changes the silhouette more than almost any other single detail.

Design notes

  • Make the cape detachable or attached at the yoke so the base garment sells on its own.
  • Cut with care; pile and sheen change with direction, so lay all pieces one way.
  • Check armhole depth and movement at fit; volume needs ease.

Fabric and trims

Viscose velvet, satin-back crepe or washable silk-feel polyester.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Contrast collars and cuffs

White collars and cuffs on coloured shirting. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Use contrast to highlight the shape; check colour fastness between shades.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.
  • Use a firm fusible or interlining so the collar holds its shape after washing.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Cropped boxy shirts

Short camp-collar shirts with straight hems. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Grade extra ease carefully so smaller sizes do not swamp.
  • Check the crop length against high-rise bottoms.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.

Fabric and trims

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Epaulette shirts

Shoulder tabs on utility shirts. The shoulder line sets the silhouette and how the garment drapes.

Design notes

  • Keep classic trench details (storm flap, epaulettes, belt) but simplify proportions for a modern look.
  • Keep utility pockets flat and tidy for city wear; avoid too much bulk.
  • Adjust shoulder pads or remove them to match the silhouette.

Fabric and trims

Cotton gabardine, coated cotton or water-repellent twill.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Floppy oversized collars

Wide soft collars borrowed from school uniforms. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Keep the collar flat and well-interfaced so it does not curl.
  • Grade extra ease carefully so smaller sizes do not swamp.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Cotton poplin, viscose twill, georgette or cotton jersey, with shell or resin buttons.

Where to use it

Womenswear, Tops & Shirts, rising

Lace yoke insets

Lace panels inset at yoke or sleeve. Trims give an instant seasonal update to proven styles.

Design notes

  • Line lace where needed and place scalloped edges at hems and cuffs.
  • Use the yoke to add shape and structure.
  • Check trim care is compatible with the main fabric.

Fabric and trims

Cotton lace, broderie anglaise or recycled polyester lace.

Where to use it

Best on blouses, poplin shirts, jersey tops and going-out tops. Works in womenswear tops and shirts as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Velvet cape tops
  • Contrast collars and cuffs
  • Cropped boxy shirts
  • Epaulette shirts
  • Floppy oversized collars
  • Lace yoke insets

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Double-pocket camp shirts
  • Gilded embroidered shirting
  • Henley plackets
  • Pleated bib fronts
  • Polo-collar knit tops
  • Slogan T-shirts

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Sheer mesh layering tops
  • Striped Breton tops
  • Wide ruffle collars

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Visible corset boning

Actions

  • Add white contrast collars to existing shirt blocks.
  • Build one striped rugby shirt in three colourways.
  • Use tipping on knit V-necks and cuffs.
  • Offer the pinstripe suit as separates so customers can mix sizes.
  • Use pointed shoulders on jackets, but keep trousers relaxed.
  • Add a waistcoat that works alone as a top.
