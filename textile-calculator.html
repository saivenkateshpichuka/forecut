About the textile calculator: units, yarn counts and where the formulas come from

What is in the textile calculator

  • Yarn: count systems, ply, length and weight, twist, strength, diameter and sett, blend price.
  • Weaving: reed and ends, warp and weft, GSM from construction, cover factor, loom-to-finished, loom production.
  • Knitting: knit GSM and tightness, circular machine needles, width and output.
  • Garment: woven and knit consumption, marker efficiency, sewing thread, shrinkage, SMV, costing to MRP.
  • Dyeing & processing: dye recipe and liquor, padding pick-up, moisture regain and invoice weight.
  • Units: every length, weight, area, GSM, density, twist, force, volume, price and temperature unit.

Units and yarn counts

Every length, width, weight, area, density and price box has its own unit menu, so you can mix units freely: a reed width in inches, a cloth length in metres and a yarn price per pound all work together. Yarn counts can be entered in any of 15 systems, from English cotton (Ne), metric (Nm), tex, dtex and denier to worsted, linen lea, American woollen run and cut, Yorkshire skein, spun silk, raw silk drams and jute. Internally every count is converted to tex, the weight in grams of 1000 metres, which keeps mixed-system calculations exact.

Where the formulas come from

The yarn, warp, weft, reed, mixing and cloth-structure calculations follow the classic rules set out in E. A. Posselt's Textile Calculations (1906), restated in modern units. For example, 12,600 yards of 30s cotton weigh 8 oz, and a 20s and a 60s twisted together make a 15s. Knitting, garment, sewing-thread, SMV, dyeing and regain calculations use the standard formulas taught in textile and apparel courses today. Each calculator shows its formula, so you can check the working.

Good to know

  • Formulas and unit conversions are exact. Consumption, thread ratios, packing factors and typical ranges are rules of thumb: confirm with your marker, your mill or a lab test before ordering bulk.
  • Wider fabric is often cheaper per garment even when the price per metre is higher: compare the fabric cost per piece.
  • GST on garments depends on the price band and can change; check the current rate.

Three worked examples

  • From a quote to a garment cost. A mill offers a 150 cm wide cotton poplin at a price per metre. Enter the width, the garment type and the size range in the consumption calculator to get metres per piece, then multiply by the price in the costing calculator. Repeat with a 112 cm wide alternative: the narrower fabric is often cheaper per metre but needs more metres per shirt, so compare the cost per piece, not the cost per metre.
  • Checking a knit's weight. A supplier says a single jersey is 180 GSM. With the width entered, the GSM calculator gives grams per running metre, which is how many knit mills sell by the kilo. If a lab test later shows 165 GSM, the same box tells you how many extra metres a kilo will give and how the garment will feel lighter.
  • Matching yarn systems. A European spinner quotes Nm 50 and an Indian mill quotes Ne 30. Convert both to tex to see that they are close but not identical, then check twist and strength before treating them as substitutes.

Regional habits worth knowing

India, Bangladesh and Pakistan mostly trade cotton yarn in Ne and fabric by the metre or kilo; Europe uses Nm for wool and linen and g/m for woven cloth; the United States still quotes denim in ounces per square yard; and East Asian filament suppliers work in denier and dtex. Because each box has its own unit menu, you can type a quote exactly as it arrives and let the calculator do the conversion.

Common questions

Why does my consumption figure differ from the factory's? The calculator gives a typical size M figure. One-way prints, nap, stripes that must match and the factory's marker efficiency all change the real number, usually upward.

Is regain included? Yes, there is a separate regain calculator for converting between oven-dry and commercial weight, useful for wool, cotton and viscose deliveries.

Can I save results? Use Copy results or Print to keep a record with your tech pack.
