The story

What is behind it, and where it was seen.

Structured clarity

Consumers move away from constant novelty toward fewer, better pieces: clean column shapes, clear lines and minimal trims.

Why now

Early SS28 trade intelligence describes a demand for "better" fashion rather than more fashion.

Key pieces

Column shapes, Clean fronts, Minimal trims; clean column dresses.

Source: FashionUnited, "Spring Summer 2028 Trend Intelligence: The Rise of Structured Clarity". Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Performance ease

Designs that feel intuitive and move easily: articulated sleeves and knees, stretch and breathable fabrics for sport and commuting.

Why now

Early SS28 previews point to performance design that removes restriction.

Key pieces

Articulation, Stretch, Breathable; articulated sleeves.

Source: FashionUnited, "SS28 Preview: The modern flow of performance design". Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Commuter tailoring

Suits built for city days: stretch suiting, soft shoulders, concealed buttons and easy-care fabrics.

Why now

Hybrid working needs tailoring that is comfortable on a commute and smart at a desk.

Key pieces

Stretch suits, Concealed buttons, Soft shoulders; stretch commuter suiting.

Source: Runway shows for this season have not happened yet. Forecut will add the brands showing this theme once collections are presented.

Design notes

How to put the leading pieces into a range.

Menswear, Outerwear, rising

Belted field coats

Field coats with a waist belt. Waist treatment controls the silhouette and how the garment flatters.

Design notes

  • Add a hidden inner tie or snap so the wrap stays closed when worn.
  • Mark waist position on the block and check on several body shapes.
  • Offer adjustable or elastic options for size inclusivity.

Fabric and trims

Wool melton, waxed or brushed cotton, recycled nylon ripstop, with matte metal zips and snaps.

Where to use it

Best on overcoats, bombers, chore jackets, puffers and overshirts. Works in menswear outerwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Outerwear, rising

Coach jackets

Snap-front nylon coach jackets. This is a garment-level idea that can anchor a capsule.

Design notes

  • Size buttons to the garment weight; use shanks on thick fabrics.
  • Start with a small capsule and track sell-through.
  • Build two price tiers of the idea.

Fabric and trims

Wool melton, waxed or brushed cotton, recycled nylon ripstop, with matte metal zips and snaps. Trims: horn-effect, corozo, metal or mother-of-pearl effect buttons.

Where to use it

Best on overcoats, bombers, chore jackets, puffers and overshirts. Works in menswear outerwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Outerwear, rising

Cropped work jackets

Short workwear jackets with chest pockets. This is a silhouette change, so it needs new blocks and fit sessions.

Design notes

  • Check the crop length against high-rise bottoms.
  • Develop the new block on a core style first, then roll out.
  • Fit on at least two sizes before committing to bulk.

Fabric and trims

Wool melton, waxed or brushed cotton, recycled nylon ripstop, with matte metal zips and snaps.

Where to use it

Menswear, Outerwear, rising

Field jackets with bellows pockets

Field jackets with expanding pockets. Pockets add function and visual interest, and are easy to vary across a range.

Design notes

  • Keep utility pockets flat and tidy for city wear; avoid too much bulk.
  • Reinforce pocket corners with bar tacks.
  • Check pocket placement against hand position on the fit model.

Fabric and trims

Cotton ripstop, gabardine or nylon twill with snap or Velcro closures.

Where to use it

Best on overcoats, bombers, chore jackets, puffers and overshirts. Works in menswear outerwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Outerwear, rising

Funnel-neck puffers

Puffers with tall funnel necks. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Plan channel width with the silhouette: wider channels look bolder, narrow channels look more refined.
  • Keep the funnel high but soft at the front so it is comfortable under the chin.
  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.

Fabric and trims

Recycled nylon or matte peached shells with recycled polyester wadding.

Where to use it

Best on overcoats, bombers, chore jackets, puffers and overshirts. Works in menswear outerwear as a capsule hero or as a quiet update to core styles.

Menswear, Outerwear, rising

Hooded parkas

Long parkas with fixed hoods. It frames the face, so it reads instantly on the hanger and in product imagery.

Design notes

  • Grade collar size with the body so it stays in proportion across sizes.
  • Use a firm fusible or interlining so the collar holds its shape after washing.

Strategy and price

What to build, test, sell through and drop, with price bands.

Build depth

Rising pieces

  • Belted field coats
  • Coach jackets
  • Cropped work jackets
  • Field jackets with bellows pockets
  • Funnel-neck puffers
  • Hooded parkas

Test small

Emerging pieces

  • Nothing at this stage

Sell through

Peaking pieces

  • Contrast-stitch nylon twill blousons
  • Fisherman-style raincoats
  • Chore and work jackets
  • Featherweight trench windbreakers
  • Leather overshirts
  • Lightweight harrington jackets

Exit or rework

Fading pieces

  • Clean leather bombers
  • Denim trucker jackets
  • Quilt-lined car coats
  • Shearling-collar aviators
  • Short suede blousons
  • Utility trench details

Actions

  • Edit ranges down and deepen buys on proven fits.
  • Upgrade fabrics on core styles.
  • Tell a "made to last" story.
  • Add stretch to core tailoring fabrics.
  • Introduce articulated elbows on outerwear.
  • Test breathability claims before marketing them.
