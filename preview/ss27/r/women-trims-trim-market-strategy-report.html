SS27 Women's Trim Market Strategy

Where to spend on trims in a low-growth, cost-pressured 2027: use trims to refresh core shapes, sell decorative trims as add-ons, and keep labour-heavy embellishment for premium pieces.

Season
Spring/Summer 2027
Audience
Women

Trims

Market
Global: New York, London, Milan, Paris and Copenhagen runways; premium and mid-market womenswear

The market context

Fashion is expected to stay in low single-digit growth in 2027, and FashionUnited's outlook argues that the question is how much growth will cost[1]. Freight, energy and financing costs remain high, and excess stock ties up cash and forces markdowns[1]. In the mid-market, promotional pressure and competition from luxury and value players squeeze margins[1]. Since 19 July 2026, large companies in the EU may not destroy certain unsold apparel, accessories and footwear[1].

In that climate, trims are a low-risk way to update a range: they change how a core piece looks without new patterns, new fabrics or new production runs.

Build, test, sell through, exit

Action Trims Why
Build Belts, ribbon ties and bows, lace edging Rising on the runway[2][3]; low cost; work across price points
Test Fringe, visible fastenings (Velcro, cross-lacing, sequinned snaps), brooches Early signals[2][3][4]; higher fashion risk
Sell through Lingerie lace, crochet trims At their height; avoid new depth
Premium only 3D petals, crystal and bead embroidery Hand labour; cost justified only at higher prices

By market

  • Europe: with the unsold-goods destruction ban[1], keep trims modular and removable so unsold stock can be reworked.
  • India and the Gulf: embroidery, lace and ribbon suit festive and modest dressing; India's economy grew 7.8% year on year in the second quarter of 2026[1].
  • United States: belts and add-on accessories support units per transaction where spending growth is modest[1].
  • Hot-climate markets: light trims (ribbon, rolled hems) suit single-layer summer clothes.

Design implications

  • Design core pieces with trim positions in mind (belt loops, ribbon channels, removable corsage pins).
  • Standardise trim suppliers across themes to raise order quantities and cut cost.

Commercial implications

  • Budget trims as a share of the cost price per tier, with hand-applied decoration kept to premium.
  • Track sell-through by trim variant and reorder the winners in season.
  • Price separately sold trims (belts, corsages, brooches) to lift the average basket.
